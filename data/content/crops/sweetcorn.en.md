## Starting
Sweetcorn loves warmth and dislikes cold, wet spring soil. Start seeds indoors from mid-April to early May. Deep pots like root trainers, soil blocks, or deep plastic pots work best. Keep them at eighteen to twenty-five degrees Celsius. Only sow or move containers outside after the Ice Saints in mid-May. Ensure the outdoor soil reaches at least ten degrees, as seeds rot quickly in chilly soil. Pre-moisten your compost before planting. Starting early indoors protects your young plants from hungry birds and mice while giving them a vital head start for our short Dutch summers.

## Seedling
Sweetcorn seedlings quickly form delicate, deep roots and strongly dislike being pricked out. Always sow directly into individual pots with one or two seeds, thinning to the strongest sprout. Plant them out into larger containers two to three weeks after sowing. Never leave seedlings cramped in small pots for too long. Rootbound plants will stall in growth and bolt prematurely. Handle their root balls with extreme care during transplanting to keep them thriving and happy.

## Vegetative
Sweetcorn requires generous container space. Use tubs of forty to sixty litres for a small cluster of three or four plants. Always arrange plants in a compact block rather than a single line. This setup guarantees good wind stability and vital pollination later. Soil must be rich, blended with mature compost and fed regularly with organic liquid fertiliser. Sweetcorn evaporates immense amounts of water through its large leaves, so never let containers dry out. Stems can reach two metres and become top-heavy. Support stems with bamboo stakes or ropes. Mound soil around the stem base to trigger sturdy aerial prop roots.

## Flowering
Each plant produces separate male and female blooms. Male tassels emerge at the top to drop pollen. Lower down, female flowers form silky threads in the leaf axils. Every single silk thread leads to one kernel and must receive pollen to fill out. Because sheltered balconies lack strong breezes, lend nature a gentle hand. Shake stems gently on sunny mornings when pollen sheds. Alternatively, snip a ripe tassel and dust it directly over the damp silks to ensure complete cobs.

## Harvest
Harvest arrives between August and September. Check when the silks turn dark brown and completely dry. The cob should feel plump and firm right to the tip. Peel back the husk slightly and press a thumbnail into a kernel. If sweet, milky sap bursts out, it is ready. Watery liquid means wait; doughy flesh means you waited too long. Traditional varieties like Golden Bantam convert sugar to starch immediately, so cook them straight away. Modern supersweet varieties stay crisp in the fridge for several days. For freezing, blanch whole cobs in boiling water for eight to ten minutes, chill in ice water, and freeze.

## FAQ
### Why are my sweetcorn cobs half empty?
Gaps on the cob stem from poor pollination. If plants grow in a straight row or during prolonged rain, pollen clumps and misses the silks. Earwigs might also chew off the silks before fertilisation occurs. Plant strictly in compact blocks and shake stems manually in the morning to dust silks thoroughly.

### Can I grow just a single sweetcorn plant in a pot?
A solitary plant will almost certainly fail to pollinate properly, leaving you with barren or missing kernels. Sweetcorn relies entirely on wind moving pollen from top tassels to silks. Group at least four to nine plants closely together in a block of containers to catch airborne pollen effectively.

### How do I protect young seedlings from birds and mice?
Pigeons and crows love tugging out fresh sprouts to eat the seed underneath, while mice dig up newly sown kernels. Pre-sow inside to bypass this fragile period completely. If planting outdoors, cover pots securely with fine mesh netting until the young plants develop strong stems and roots.

### Why did my sweetcorn plants fall over in the wind?
Sweetcorn plants grow tall and top-heavy with relatively shallow roots, making them vulnerable to sudden summer storms. Protect balcony pots by staking individual stems with sturdy bamboo or wrapping securing cords around the plant cluster. Mounding compost around the stems encourages anchoring aerial prop roots.

### Why does my homegrown sweetcorn taste starchy and bland?
Sweetcorn easily cross-pollinates with agricultural forage maize if a field is within one to two hundred metres. Incoming field maize pollen turns sweet kernels starchy and mealy. Harvest timing also matters; picking too late or leaving traditional varieties sitting at room temperature quickly converts natural sugars into starch.

### Can I prick out sweetcorn seedlings into bigger trays?
No, sweetcorn handles root disturbance very poorly. Pricking out or separating tangled root systems stalls growth and causes premature bolting. Always sow directly into individual modules, deep pots, or soil blocks. Thin by snipping the weaker seedling, and transfer the intact root ball carefully into its final container.

### How often should I water sweetcorn in containers?
Containers dry out rapidly, and corn transpires massive amounts of moisture through its wide leaves. Check the soil daily and keep it consistently moist, especially during tasselling, flowering, and kernel formation. Drought stress during pollination will dry out silks, severely reducing your harvest.

### When is the right time to sow outside in the Netherlands?
Wait until the Ice Saints pass in mid-May before planting or moving pots outside. The soil and air temperatures must reach at least ten degrees Celsius. Seeds planted in cold, wet spring earth will rot instead of germinating, making early indoor starts in April far safer.

## Benefits
Enjoy freshly picked sweetcorn immediately for peak sweetness. Strip away the outer leaves, but leave tender inner husks attached to lock in moisture. Bring unsalted water to a boil; never add salt to the pot, as salt toughens the delicate skins. Simmer covered for eight to fifteen minutes. Serve the steaming cobs slathered in rich butter, flaky sea salt, and paprika. Alternatively, char them briefly over a hot grill or barbecue until the kernels turn golden and lightly caramelised.
