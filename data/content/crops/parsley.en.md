## Starting
Choose between curly parsley for textural garnish or flat-leaf varieties like 'Amsterdamse Snij' for robust culinary flavour. Parsley seeds germinate notoriously slowly due to natural oils. Soak them in lukewarm water for twenty-four hours to cut sprouting time down to ten to fourteen days. Start indoors from February to April at 15 to 20 °C, or sow outside from late spring through August. Cover the seeds lightly with sharp sand or seed compost, keeping the surface consistently moist. Young indoor starts tolerate cold well. Harden them off carefully and move them outside after IJsheiligen in mid-May.

## Seedling
Parsley quickly forms a delicate taproot that resents disturbance. Pricking out individual plants causes transplant shock and triggers premature bolting. Instead, practice multi-sowing by grouping four to ten seeds in each small cell or pot. Once roots fill the plug, transplant the entire cluster intact. Direct-seeded pots can simply be thinned into small clusters. Right after emergence, move your indoor seedlings to a bright, cool room kept at 12 to 15 °C. This cool light keeps the young stems strong, compact, and sturdy.

## Vegetative
Give your container parsley a pot at least 20 to 25 cm deep and wide, fitted with drainage holes to protect its deep taproot. Parsley thrives best in partial shade. Harsh midsummer sun scorches leaves, turns them yellow, and induces bolting. Use a moisture-retentive, humus-rich potting mix amended with mature compost. Avoid fresh manure, unripe compost, and high salts, which rot roots. Keep the soil consistently cool and moist. In hot balcony weather, water daily at the base of the plant to prevent leaf fungi. Feed lightly with a dilute organic liquid fertiliser once a month during peak growth, skipping heavy nitrogen.

## Flowering
Parsley is a biennial crop. It produces lush culinary leaves during its first season and overwinters cleanly. Longer days and warming spring weather in year two trigger central flower stalks. Once this blooming phase begins, leaf production halts, and foliage turns tough, fibrous, and bitter. Allow the yellow-green flower umbels to bloom if you want to support visiting hoverflies, bees, and predatory wasps. Seeds dry to brown in late summer for collection. For continuous tender culinary greens, always start a fresh pot every spring.

## Harvest
Start harvesting once your plant has established sturdy upright stems. Always cut outer stems near the soil line using sharp snips. Leave the central growing heart completely undisturbed so the plant continuously produces fresh foliage across summer, autumn, and mild winters. Keep cut stems fresh indoors by placing them upright in a glass of water, or store them loosely wrapped in a damp paper towel within your refrigerator drawer. For longer storage, always choose freezing over drying, as dehydration ruins delicate essential oils. Wash, spin dry, chop the foliage finely, and pack it into airtight containers or ice cube trays topped with olive oil.

## FAQ
### Why are my parsley leaves turning reddish-orange and wilting?
This damage is typically caused by carrot fly larvae burrowing into the taproots. As root tissue is destroyed beneath the soil line, foliage turns reddish-orange, wilts, and collapses. Willow-carrot aphids also cause discoloured, mottled yellow and purple leaves by transmitting viruses.

### How do I protect balcony parsley from carrot fly?
You can easily prevent carrot fly attacks on a balcony by elevating your containers. Carrot flies rarely fly higher than sixty to eighty centimetres above ground level. Alternatively, cover your parsley clusters with fine insect netting measuring 1 to 1.3 millimetres to block egg-laying adults completely.

### Why are my parsley leaves scorching and turning yellow in summer?
Intense direct sunlight, dried-out roots, and heat stress quickly turn parsley foliage yellow. Parsley thrives best in partial shade. Position your container where it receives gentle morning light or dappled shade, and keep the compost evenly moist by watering consistently around the base of the plant.

### Why have my parsley seeds not emerged after three weeks?
Parsley seeds naturally take three to four weeks to sprout, particularly in cool soil below 12 °C. If the surface dries out even once, germination fails. Soak seeds in lukewarm water for twenty-four hours beforehand, and keep the soil covered with moist fleece or jute burlap until green shoots appear.

### What type of soil and fertiliser does container parsley need?
Parsley requires open, moisture-retentive, humus-rich soil amended with well-rotted compost. Avoid fresh manure, unripe compost, and excessive salts, as they trigger root rot. Because pot nutrients wash out with watering, feed once a month during peak growth with a dilute organic liquid feed. Skip heavy nitrogen to keep aroma concentrated.

### Can I plant parsley where carrots or celery grew recently?
Parsley belongs to the Apiaceae family alongside carrots, celery, celeriac, parsnips, and dill. In small garden beds, observe a three- to four-year crop rotation break to avoid soil-borne pests. If growing on a balcony, simply plant into fresh potting mix to bypass rotation restrictions entirely.

### Should I choose curly parsley or flat-leaf parsley?
Choose flat-leaf parsley like 'Amsterdamse Snij' or 'Gigante d’Italia' if you want rich, deep, savory flavour for warm sauces, marinades, and stews. Curly parsley offers a milder, fresher grassy taste with a crisp texture, making it ideal as a delicate garnish, for potato salads, or in classic herb blends.

### Can parsley survive Dutch winters outside on a balcony?
Parsley is cold-hardy and easily overwinters on sheltered balconies, especially if sown late in summer or placed in a cold greenhouse for winter picking. In its second spring, warming temperatures cause the plant to produce flower stalks. Sow a fresh batch annually to maintain tender leaves for cooking.

### Why is freezing parsley recommended instead of drying it?
Drying parsley destroys the delicate volatile essential oils, leaving behind brittle leaves with very little fragrance or flavour. Freezing preserves taste and texture far better. Finely chop your freshly washed leaves and pack them tightly into airtight containers or freeze them in ice cube trays with a splash of water or oil.

## Benefits
Both parsley types shine in home cooking. Use savory flat-leaf parsley for pestos, chimichurri, marinades, and tabbouleh. Reserve curly parsley for potato salads, fines herbes blends, or crisp garnishes. As a golden culinary rule, add chopped parsley at the very end of cooking or right before serving. Boiling destroys its volatile essential oils and creates a bitter taste. Never discard tender stems. Chop them finely alongside leaves for aromatic crunch, or tie whole stems with thyme and bay leaf into a classic bouquet garni.
