## Starting
Autumn planting is ideal for your Dutch balcony or small garden. Plant individual cloves between mid-October and mid-November. Garlic needs winter cold to trigger the bulb to divide into separate cloves. Position each clove with its flat root plate downward and the pointed tip pointing upward. Spring planting is possible between February and early April, though bulbs will remain smaller or stay single-clove. Always select certified seed cloves rather than supermarket bulbs. Use frost-resistant containers made of terracotta, wood, or sturdy plastic with generous drainage holes. Fill them with light, airy, free-draining soil so cloves never sit in standing water.

## Seedling
Garlic needs no traditional seed starting or pricking out. You plant cloves directly at their final positions in your container. Roots form quickly within three to four weeks beneath the soil. Green shoots often pop up before winter arrives. These young shoots typically reach around five to ten centimetres tall. Do not worry about cold weather harming them. At this size, the young plants overwinter safely without losing any cold hardiness.

## Vegetative
Provide containers with a depth of at least twenty to twenty-five centimetres and clear drainage holes. Garlic dislikes wet feet. Normal Dutch rainfall is enough during autumn and winter, though pots under roof eaves need occasional light sips. From April to June, keep the soil consistently moist as the bulbs swell. Stop watering two to three weeks before harvest. Garlic needs moderate feeding. Never apply fresh manure or high-nitrogen fertilizer, which encourages fungal leaf problems and invites onion flies. Instead, mix in well-rotted compost or apply an organic fertilizer rich in potassium in early spring. The upright leaves need no extra support.

## Flowering
Hardneck garlic varieties produce curled flower stalks, known as scapes, in late spring. Snap or cut these stems off as soon as they curl. Allowing flowers or small bulbils to develop diverts vital energy away from the underground bulb, reducing your final harvest size. Do not throw these curled stems into the compost. Excised scapes carry a delicious, mild garlic flavour and are wonderful additions to your kitchen dishes.

## Harvest
Harvest autumn-planted garlic from late June to mid-July. Watch the foliage carefully. Lift your bulbs when the bottom two or three leaves turn brown and dry while four or five upper leaves remain green. Leaving bulbs in wet soil too long ruins their protective wrappers. Loosen the soil gently with a hand fork and lift each bulb by its base. Brush off loose soil, but never wash the bulbs with water. Cure them in a dry, warm, shaded, airy spot for two to three weeks. Once papery, cut stems slightly above the bulb and store in a cool, dark, dry space.

## FAQ
### Can I plant garlic cloves from the supermarket?
Dutch advisors recommend against using supermarket cloves. Store bulbs often receive anti-sprouting treatments and come from warmer climates, making them poorly suited to cold Dutch winters. They can also introduce destructive fungal diseases like white rot or viral infections into your soil. Always purchase certified seed garlic for healthy crops.

### How should I protect my balcony garlic from severe frost?
Garlic easily handles temperatures down to minus ten degrees in open ground. However, container roots can freeze solid during prolonged cold spells. Move your pots against a sheltered house wall when hard freezes strike, or wrap them in horticultural fleece. By IJsheiligen in mid-May, frost risks pass completely.

### Why are my garlic leaves turning yellow?
Yellowing lower leaves in mid-spring can signal temporary waterlogging, a nitrogen deficiency, or dry periods. Check that your container drains freely. Later in June, yellowing foliage is completely natural. It shows that your garlic is finishing its life cycle and directing its final energy into the bulbs.

### What causes small orange spots on garlic leaves?
These bright orange-red pustules are garlic rust, a fungal condition appearing during warm, humid spring weather. Prevent rust by spacing plants well to improve air circulation and avoiding overhead watering. Practise a four-to-six-year crop rotation with allium plants in your garden beds to prevent spores from returning.

### How often should I water garlic in pots?
Garlic hates wet soil. Autumn and winter rainfall in the Netherlands provides plenty of moisture. Pots tucked under eaves only need an occasional sip so the soil never goes bone-dry. Increase watering from April to June as the bulbs swell, then stop watering completely two weeks before harvesting.

### Can I plant garlic in the spring instead of autumn?
Yes, you can plant cloves from February until early April. However, garlic needs winter cold to trigger proper bulb division into separate cloves. Spring plantings produce noticeably smaller bulbs or single-clove rounds. Autumn planting remains the best method for full, multi-clove heads.

### What is the best container for growing garlic?
Choose sturdy, frost-resistant pots made from terracotta, wood, or heavy-duty plastic. Ensure your containers have plenty of bottom drainage holes and a minimum depth of twenty to twenty-five centimetres. This provides ample space for healthy root growth while keeping the soil airy and completely free of standing water.

### What fertilizer works best for growing garlic?
Garlic requires low-to-moderate nutrients. Never feed it fresh manure or high-nitrogen fertilizers. These encourage soft, disease-prone leaves and attract onion flies. Instead, blend well-rotted compost into your potting mix before planting. You can also apply an organic fertilizer containing potassium in early spring to support bulb swelling.

## Benefits
Homegrown garlic brings exceptional versatility to your cooking. Harvest early green garlic in May for a juicy, mild flavour you can slice without peeling. Late spring scapes are prized for grilling, tossing into stir-fries, or blending into rich pesto. Once cured, the mature cloves offer deep, robust flavour whether crushed raw into dressings or slowly roasted. You can also combine your freshly harvested cloves with garden herbs to create fragrant infused oils and flavorful culinary marinades.
