## Starting
Mustard greens thrive in the Dutch maritime climate and suit balcony pots or small garden boxes well. You can sow seeds outdoors from March through October. Cold tolerance allows sowing long before the Ice Saints in mid-May. For an early start, sow under cold glass in February, then sow directly outside from March. The finest results arrive in early spring or late summer, avoiding hot summer bolting. Choose an airy potting mix or humus-rich compost. Ensure your container receives four to six hours of sunlight daily, though partial shade works well in warmer months. Seeds germinate within six to ten days at soil temperatures between 10 °C and 20 °C.

## Seedling
These fast-growing greens sprout quickly on your balcony. Sowing directly into containers is ideal. If you want tender baby leaves, sow them closely together in your planter box. For full-grown plants and expansive leaves like Red Giant, simply thin your seedlings down. Skip delicate pricking out altogether to save time. The seedlings you remove during thinning make a delicious, spicy microgreen harvest. Bring them straight into the kitchen and enjoy them immediately on snacks or in early fresh salads.

## Vegetative
Use pots with a volume of five to ten liters per plant and generous drainage holes. Add pebbles or potsherds lined with cloth to prevent root rot. Mustard greens reach a height of twenty to forty centimeters and need no physical support. Keep your potting soil consistently lightly moist at all times. Drought stress causes leaves to turn fibrous and excessively hot, while also triggering early flowering. Mix compost into the soil before planting your crop. When growing repeatedly in balcony containers, feed with a mild organic liquid plant fertilizer every few weeks to encourage fresh, tender foliage.

## Flowering
During flowering, the plant produces bright yellow cruciferous blooms rich in pollen and nectar. These blossoms quickly attract wild bees, butterflies, and hoverflies to your balcony. Once flowering begins, vegetative leaf growth slows down while the foliage turns notably tougher and more pungent. After pollination, elongated seed pods develop on the stalks. Allow these pods to dry out fully on the plant to gather round seeds. You can save them for sowing next season or grind them up for homemade mustard.

## Harvest
Harvest baby leaves after three to five weeks by cutting foliage just above the soil. Leave the central heart intact, and plants will regrow two to three times. For larger harvests, pick the outer leaves when mature and let the inner center continue producing. Mustard greens easily handle light to moderate night frosts, offering fresh winter greens through December or January in a cold frame. Store unwashed leaves in a ventilated container in the refrigerator for five to seven days. Alternatively, wash leaves and roll them loosely in a damp tea towel inside the crisper drawer for two to three days.

## FAQ
### How do I prevent flea beetles from damaging my mustard greens?
Flea beetles are tiny jumping beetles that bite small pinholes into leaves. They strongly dislike moisture. Keep the soil and foliage consistently damp by misting or gently sprinkling water. You can also mulch your pots or protect your container plants completely with fine insect netting.

### What should I do if caterpillars attack the leaves?
Large and small cabbage white butterflies lay eggs on mustard greens because they are part of the cabbage family. Protect your containers with fine insect mesh or a garden fleece cover. You can also inspect the leaves regularly and pick off visible eggs and caterpillars by hand.

### How can I protect young seedlings from slugs?
Young seedlings are especially vulnerable to hungry slugs on balconies and in gardens. Keep your containers tidy by removing dead leaves promptly. Inspect your pots during the evening hours, and hand-pick any slugs you find resting on the soil or container rims before they cause harm.

### Why are my mustard greens bolting and flowering so quickly?
Mustard greens quickly bolt and produce flower stalks in early summer due to long daylight hours and high temperatures. To avoid this, time your sowings for the cool early spring before May. Alternatively, pause during midsummer and start your autumn crops from August onwards.

### Can mustard greens survive the cold Dutch winter outdoors?
Yes, mustard greens handle light to moderate night frost quite well. In a cold frame or greenhouse tunnel, you can continue harvesting fresh leaves well into December or January. Their natural resilience makes them an outstanding leafy crop for extending your seasonal growing window.

### How much sunlight do container mustard greens require daily?
Mustard greens need four to six hours of sunlight per day for strong growth. During warmer weather periods, placing containers in partial shade works very well. This slight protection prevents drought stress and helps stop the plants from developing overly harsh, fibrous leaves too quickly.

### How do I store freshly picked mustard greens properly?
You can store harvested leaves inside a ventilated, sealed container in the refrigerator for five to seven days. Another method is to rinse the leaves and wrap them loosely inside a slightly damp tea towel. Stored in your vegetable crisper drawer, they remain crisp for two to three days.

### How often should I fertilize mustard greens in pots?
Begin by mixing rich compost into the potting soil when planting. If you plan on multiple successive harvests from the same pot, supply a mild dose of organic liquid fertilizer every few weeks. This feeding routine sustains vigorous leaf regrowth without overburdening the shallow root system.

## Benefits
Mustard greens bring a vibrant kick to your kitchen creations. Young leaves offer a fresh, horseradish-like spice that enlivens mixed raw salads, sandwiches, and cold lunch wraps. As the foliage matures, the leaves hold up wonderfully to warm cooking. Stir-fry them briefly in a wok, braise them lightly, or fold them straight into a traditional Dutch stamppot. Heating the greens mellows their sharp bite, leaving behind a delightfully tender, mild, and aromatic cabbage flavor that pairs well with savory meals.
