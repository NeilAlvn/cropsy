## Starting
Celery is a slow starter that needs patience on your balcony. Sow seeds indoors from early March to April at a steady 18 °C to 20 °C. The tiny seeds require light to sprout. Sprinkle them over moist seed compost and barely cover them with a whisper of fine sand or vermiculite. Expect germination to take two to three weeks. You can also regrow store-bought celery. Cut two to three centimetres from the base. Let it sit in a shallow dish of lukewarm water on a bright windowsill. Once roots and fresh leaves emerge, transplant it straight into a pot with potting soil.

## Seedling
Move seedlings into individual pots with compost once true leaves appear. Keep them warm at a minimum of 15 °C. Cold temperatures under 10 °C to 12 °C will trigger premature flowering later. After mid-May passes, bring your pots outdoors. Harden plants off for a week by bringing them inside every night. When planting into their final container, keep the central heart level with the soil. Planting too deep causes rot, while planting too high dries the roots out.

## Vegetative
Choose a container at least 30 centimetres deep with drainage holes. Provide ten to fifteen litres of humus-rich soil per plant, mixed with compost or well-rotted manure. Celery has shallow roots and loses water quickly. Keep the soil constantly moist. Dry spells cause hollow, tough, and fibrous stalks. Always pour water at the base of the stems, never over the foliage. Feed regularly with diluted liquid plant food or nettle liquid to fuel lush growth. If growing traditional non-self-blanching varieties, wrap stalks in cardboard or newspaper two to three weeks before harvesting to soften stems and reduce bitterness.

## Flowering
Celery naturally blooms in its second summer, producing delicate white flower umbels. However, cold spring temperatures below 10 °C or severe drought can trigger premature bolting in year one. Bolted stems turn woody and lose quality. You can still eat the flowers as a zesty garnish. They also attract many beneficial pollinators to your balcony. If you let the umbels ripen, collect the brown seeds. Stored properly, these seeds retain good germination power for five to six years.

## Harvest
Harvest celery from August well into November or December. Simply slice off the outer stalks as needed, allowing the central heart to keep producing new stems. Alternatively, cut the entire bunch just above the soil line. Keep fresh stalks crisp for up to a week by wrapping them in a damp towel in the fridge. For winter protection, cover balcony containers with thick straw or fleece against light frost. You can also freeze sliced stems raw or after blanching them for two minutes. Grind dried leaves with coarse sea salt to make aromatic homemade celery salt.

## FAQ
### Why are my celery stalks becoming hollow and tough?
Hollow, fibrous, or tough stalks are almost always caused by uneven watering or drought stress. Celery has shallow roots and loses moisture rapidly through its leafy canopy. Ensure your container soil stays consistently moist throughout warm spells. Never let the soil dry out completely between waterings.

### How do I protect celery against leaf spot disease?
Leaf spot, caused by Septoria, shows up as tiny brown spots across the leaves during warm, humid weather. Prevent it by watering exclusively at the base of the plant in the morning so foliage stays dry. Ensure adequate ventilation around containers, and quickly pluck off infected foliage.

### Can I grow a new celery plant from store scraps?
Yes, regrowing kitchen scraps is very simple. Slice off two to three centimetres from the base of a store-bought bunch. Place the base in a small dish of lukewarm water on your windowsill. Once fresh roots and young leaves sprout, pot it into nutritious potting soil.

### Why did my celery bolt into flower during its first year?
Celery is naturally a biennial plant, but stress causes premature bolting. Prolonged cold exposure below 10 °C to 12 °C during the seedling stage or severe summer drought triggers flowering early. If this happens, stalks turn woody, though the white flower umbels remain edible.

### Why should I wear gloves when harvesting celery in the sun?
Celery plant sap contains natural compounds called furocoumarins. When sap gets onto your skin during bright sunlight, it can cause phototoxic skin reactions, including redness or blisters. Protect your hands and arms by wearing gloves whenever you handle or harvest plants in direct sun.

### How deep should I plant celery seedlings in containers?
Position the seedling so that the central growing heart sits precisely level with the soil surface. Planting too deeply will suffocate the crown and cause fatal rotting. Planting too high leaves roots exposed to drying air, causing unnecessary moisture stress.

### Do I need to blanch my celery stalks before harvesting?
That depends on your variety. Modern cultivars like Tango and Golden Plume are self-blanching and do not need covering. Traditional varieties should be wrapped with cardboard, newspaper, or mounded soil for two to three weeks before harvest to reduce bitter notes and keep stalks tender.

### How can I protect celery plants from garden slugs?
Tender young celery seedlings and juicy stems are favorite targets for garden slugs. To keep your balcony containers safe, place protective physical barriers or stick copper tape around the rims of your pots. Always inspect your plants regularly to intercept slugs early.

## Benefits
Both the crunchy stems and vibrant leaves offer intense herbal flavor. Enjoy raw stalks sliced into crisp salads, snacks with dips, or blended into juices. Diced celery forms the classic mirepoix and soffritto aromatic base alongside onions and carrots for soups, stews, and bolognese sauces. It brings rich taste to stir-fries, ribollita, and mussel pots. Chop the leafy tops at the very last minute into hot broths, or blend them into an aromatic green pesto as a tasty alternative to basil.
