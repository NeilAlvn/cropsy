## Starting
Start indoors from February in seed trays at warm room temperature. Strawberries germinate slowly and may vary from the parent plant. Many gardeners prefer young runners or potted plants instead. Late summer planting in August gives the best yield next season, though spring planting also produces sweet summer berries. For balconies, everbearing varieties like Ostara or Charlotte are wonderful choices because they fruit continuously through October. June-bearers like Elsanta yield one concentrated early summer harvest. Choose containers with generous drainage holes to prevent standing water. Ensure your balcony spot catches plenty of warm sunshine throughout the day.

## Seedling
Prick out delicate seedlings into individual pots once their first true leaves appear. Pay close attention to planting depth. The crown, where leaf stems meet, must sit exactly level with the soil surface. Burying the growing heart under dirt leads to rotting. Setting it too high exposes the root neck and surface roots to drying balcony winds. Handle fragile root systems with gentle care. Keep the soil evenly moist during this vulnerable stage.

## Vegetative
Give each strawberry plant its own roomy pot filled with airy, humus-rich soil mixed with compost and perlite. A slightly acidic to neutral mix works best. Position containers where they receive at least six to seven hours of direct sunlight. Water steadily at the root base to keep foliage dry and prevent fungal issues. Feed lightly with organic fertiliser rich in potassium rather than excess nitrogen, which only creates lush leaves. Lay a clean bed of straw or cocoa shells across the soil. Prune away all emerging runners immediately so the plant channels its energy into strong growth.

## Flowering
Blossoms emerge from May in cheerful white or pink clusters. While the plants survive winter, open flowers freeze easily if late night frosts strike around the Ice Saints. Cover them with protective fleece or move containers near the building wall overnight. Frost damage turns flower centres black, preventing fruit formation. Bees and wind ensure even pollination; poor pollination causes bumpy, misshapen berries. For newly planted everbearers, pinch off the very first blossoms so roots establish fully before fruit sets.

## Harvest
Pick strawberries only when fully red and richly fragrant. They do not ripen further once harvested. Snip or snap each berry cleanly with its leafy calyx and a short stem intact. This stops juice loss and protects delicate flesh from bruising. Enjoy your harvest fresh from the pot on the same day if possible. Otherwise, store berries in an open container in the fridge for up to two days. Take them out half an hour before eating so their sweet room-temperature aroma returns. Net containers early to stop blackbirds and pigeons from stealing ripening fruit.

## FAQ
### Why are my harvested strawberries misshapen?
Uneven or bumpy strawberries usually stem from incomplete pollination during flowering. Strawberry blossoms need insects like bees or a gentle breeze to transfer pollen evenly. Cold or rainy weather keeps pollinators away, causing irregular fruit development. Ensure pots sit in an airy, sunny outdoor spot.

### How do I protect blossoms from late spring frost?
Open blossoms are vulnerable to frost around the mid-May Ice Saints. Freezing temperatures turn the flower centres black, which stops fruit from setting. Move portable balcony pots against a sheltered wall or indoors on frosty nights. Alternatively, drape horticultural fleece snugly over containers until morning sunshine returns.

### Why do I need to cut off runners during the summer?
Strawberries naturally send out long runners to create daughter plants. Growing these runners takes massive energy away from fruit development. Unless you deliberately want to propagate replacements, prune all runners off near the main plant throughout the growing season. This ensures all vital nutrients support plump, sweet berries.

### What is the best way to propagate new plants from runners?
In July and August, guide the first baby plantlet on a runner into a small container filled with soil beside the mother. Keep it watered until strong roots form. Once well-rooted, snip the connecting stem cleanly. This yields vigorous, free plants ready for autumn potting.

### Why are my strawberries rotting on the plant?
Grey mould often develops when berries rest directly on damp soil or endure prolonged rain. Prevent rot by spreading a thick layer of clean straw or cocoa shells beneath the foliage. Always water directly onto the soil rather than over leaves, and promptly remove any damaged fruit.

### Should I choose everbearing or June-bearing strawberry plants?
For small gardens and balconies, everbearing varieties like Ostara or Charlotte are ideal because they flower and produce berries continuously from June through October. June-bearing varieties like Elsanta produce one massive, concentrated harvest over three to four weeks in early summer before finishing for the season.

### How often should I replace my strawberry plants?
Strawberry plants are perennials, but their productivity declines sharply after their third harvest year. Older plants yield smaller berries and far fewer fruit clusters. Root new daughter plantlets from summer runners every couple of years so you always have fresh, high-yielding stock ready to replace declining containers.

### How do I keep birds and snails away from my berries?
Pigeons and blackbirds love red berries, so cover containers with fine netting as soon as fruits begin colouring. For snails and slugs, stick copper tape around the pot rims to create a barrier. A dry bed of straw or cocoa shells also deters creeping pests from climbing up.

## Benefits
Fresh balcony strawberries shine on Dutch rusks, pancakes, or in breakfast yogurt. They pair wonderfully in savoury salads with spinach, lamb's lettuce, and balsamic vinegar. Because strawberries naturally contain very little pectin, homemade jams need gelling sugar, lemon juice, or additions like rhubarb, apples, or redcurrants to set firmly. While thawed berries soften, frozen berries blend smoothly into coulis and smoothies. You can also freeze whole berries inside ice cubes for a pretty, refreshing touch in cold summer water.
