## Starting
Peultjes thrive in the cool Dutch spring. These cold-hardy climbers sprout best between 7 °C and 15 °C. Start indoors from late January through February. You can sow directly outside from March through April, or early August for an autumn harvest. Never wait for IJsheiligen in mid-May. Late plantings suffer from powdery mildew and tough pods when summer warmth arrives. To avoid seeds rotting in soggy spring soil, try pre-germinating them first. Fold your seeds inside damp paper towels at room temperature for three to four days. Once the root tip appears, carefully tuck them into your container trellis setup.

## Seedling
Young legumes possess fragile taproots and hate root disturbance. Never prick them out. Instead, start seeds in deep modular plug trays or cardboard toilet paper rolls. These allow the taproot to grow straight downward. When plants stand 5 to 6 cm tall in March or April, plant the entire cardboard roll directly into your outdoor planter. The sturdy little seedlings easily survive night frost and light snow. Simply drape garden fleece over them during extreme winter cold spells.

## Vegetative
Choose containers at least 20 to 25 cm deep with generous drainage holes. Balcony planters hold six to eight plants comfortably. Keep the soil lightly and evenly moist throughout growth. Skip heavy manure, fresh compost, and rich nitrogen feeds entirely. Peultjes partner with root bacteria to draw their own nitrogen from the air. Too much added fertilizer creates dense foliage with very few flowers. Provide reliable climbing support right from the start. Low bush varieties lean happily against small twigs or brushwood. Taller climbing types require netting, bamboo canes, or trellises up to two meters high. Guide the tender early shoots until their curling tendrils latch on firmly.

## Flowering
Delicate white or soft purple blossoms appear six to eight weeks after sowing. These butterfly-shaped blooms pollinate themselves, although neighborhood bees will still visit them regularly. Water consistency is vital once the flower buds arrive. Never let the container dry out completely at this stage. Dry roots cause blossoms to drop and make developing pods tough or fibrous. Water evenly around the container base. Flat, tender pods form quickly, expanding within ten to fourteen days after the petals drop.

## Harvest
Pick your peultjes while the pods remain completely flat, sweet, and tender. Do not let the interior seeds swell, or the pods become stringy and tough. Harvest every two to three days to keep vines producing fresh blooms. Always snip stems with small scissors or pinch them with your fingernails. Never yank, because fragile pea vines tear easily from their roots. Eat them fresh, or store unwashed pods in the refrigerator crisper for up to five days. You can also blanch them briefly to freeze. When harvest finishes, cut vines at soil level so decomposing roots release stored nitrogen into your potting mix.

## FAQ
### Do peultjes need to wait until after IJsheiligen?
No, peultjes are cool-weather crops that must be planted long before IJsheiligen in mid-May. Sowing them late pushes their growth into hot summer weather. Heat triggers premature powdery mildew, causes poor pod set, and makes your harvest tough and fibrous.

### How do I protect young seedlings from birds?
Pigeons and sparrows love pulling up tender pea sprouts to eat the seeds attached underneath. Shield your balcony pots or garden beds with protective wire mesh, a cloche, or insect netting. Keep this barrier in place until the plants grow twenty to thirty centimeters tall.

### Why should I pre-germinate peultjes seeds?
Pre-sprouting seeds on damp paper towels at room temperature speeds up germination. It also prevents seeds from rotting in cold, waterlogged spring soil outdoors. In addition, starting sprouted seeds inside keeps garden mice from digging them up out of outdoor pots or cold frames.

### What kind of fertilizer should I use for container peultjes?
Avoid heavy compost, animal manure, and nitrogen-heavy fertilizers. Peultjes live in symbiosis with beneficial root bacteria that capture nitrogen directly from the air. Supplying extra nitrogen causes lush leafy foliage but ruins flower and pod development. Standard peat-free potting compost provides all the nutrients they need.

### How do I prevent powdery mildew on my plants?
Powdery mildew appears as white dust on leaves during warm, dry, or humid weather late in the season. Prevent it by sowing early in spring rather than late. Ensure good air circulation between plants, and always water at the container base rather than wetting the foliage.

### Can I prick out peultjes seedlings into bigger pots?
Avoid classic pricking out completely. Legumes possess delicate taproots that dislike being disturbed. Instead, sow seeds directly into deep modular trays or cardboard toilet paper rolls. This encourages deep taproot growth and lets you plant the whole biodegradable roll outdoors without damaging sensitive roots.

### How often should I harvest the pods?
Harvest your pods every two to three days. Frequent picking prevents the pods from swelling and turning stringy. It also signals the vine to produce fresh flowers and new pods continuously. Always cut or pinch the stems gently to avoid tearing the brittle vines.

### What should I do with the plants when production ends?
When your plants finish producing in mid-summer, cut the haulms off right at the soil surface. Never pull the roots out of the container. The root nodules stay behind in the potting mix, decomposing naturally and enriching the soil with organic nitrogen for your next crop.

## Benefits
Peultjes bring crisp texture and fresh sweetness to the kitchen. Snap the top stalk and gently pull downward to de-string each pod before cooking. Keep cooking times very brief to preserve their vibrant green color and crunch. Stir-fry them for two to three minutes with garlic and ginger, or boil them for just two minutes. You can also slice raw pods into crisp spring salads. Don't forget the tender young leafy tendrils; these edible shoots make delicious, sweet garnishes.
