## Starting
Chives are wonderful for Dutch balconies. Sow seeds outdoors from February to April, or indoors in February and March. Cold weather helps break their dormancy. Sow seeds in small clusters rather than singly. Keep in mind that seeds lose their viability after one or two years. Germination takes two to three weeks, so please be patient. Growing from seed produces a modest harvest in the first year. For quick results, you can buy a small potted clump, rescue a supermarket plant, or divide an older clump in early spring. Both potted plants and divided clumps re-sprout vigorously.

## Seedling
When potting on, always move your chives in small clumps rather than individual strands. Use airy, nutritious herb potting compost or a light compost mix. Chives are completely hardy perennials. Unlike tender herbs like basil, you do not need to wait for the mid-May Ice Saints. Potted plants can live outside from March. If you raised your seedlings indoors, give them a short hardening off period before moving them to your balcony.

## Vegetative
Place your pot in full sun or light afternoon shade. Light shade keeps the hollow stems tender and delays early bolting. Use a container with a diameter and depth of at least fifteen to twenty centimetres per clump. Always ensure drainage holes are present, as standing water rots the bulbs. Chives thrive in fertile, humus-rich, and slightly limed soil. Stir some ripe compost or organic fertilizer into the pot. Keep the soil evenly moist. Extended dry spells make stems tough, yellow, and trigger flowering. Chive stems grow twenty to forty centimetres tall and remain sturdy on their own without support.

## Flowering
From May until July or August, chives produce globe-shaped, purple-pink flowers. These blooms brim with nectar, attracting bumblebees, solitary bees, and hoverflies to your balcony. However, flowering stems turn stiff, hard, and bitter. Clip flower buds off early to keep harvesting fresh, tender green stems. If you prefer blossoms, let them bloom and collect the black seeds later, or allow self-seeding. Simply trim away dried flowering stems when they finish.

## Harvest
Harvest chives continuously from March through October. Use sharp scissors to cut entire stems two to three centimetres above soil level. Never just pinch off top tips. Full cuts stimulate the clump to push up tender new shoots quickly from the base. Do not dry your harvest, as chives completely lose their aroma and essential oils. Instead, freeze chopped chives in containers, bags, or ice cube trays with a little water or oil. You can also steep the edible purple blossoms in white wine vinegar or apple cider vinegar. This creates a striking pink vinegar with a subtle onion note.

## FAQ
### What causes orange spots on my chive stems?
These orange or brownish-yellow pustules are chive rust, a common fungal disease. Do not let it spread across your container. Cut the entire clump down to ground level immediately. Dispose of diseased stems in your trash rather than home compost. Your chives will quickly sprout clean, healthy new stems.

### How do I deal with black aphids on chives?
Black aphids sometimes gather in dense chive stems during late spring. You can rinse them away using a firm jet of water from a hose or spray bottle. If the infestation is heavy, cut the plant down close to the soil. Chives recover fast and regrow fresh foliage.

### Why is my chive plant losing vigour after several years?
Chive clumps naturally become overcrowded and exhausted after two to four years. In early spring, lift the root ball out of the pot. Divide the clump into several pieces by pulling it apart. Replant the most vigorous sections into fresh soil with compost to restore strong growth.

### Can I plant supermarket potted chives outdoors?
Yes, supermarket chives are very resilient. Discarded or tired grocery pots will easily revive when planted in a balcony container. Plant the clump into airy, compost-enriched soil and water it well. Cut down old stems, and the root system will quickly push up crisp new growth.

### Can I dry chives for winter storage?
No, drying chives is not recommended. Drying strips away their volatile essential oils and delicate onion flavour, leaving them tasteless. For the best preservation, finely chop fresh stems and freeze them in sealed containers or ice cube trays with water. You can also infuse blossoms into vinegar.

### Why are my chive stems tough and bitter?
Stems become hard, woody, and bitter when the plant flowers. Cutting off flower buds early keeps foliage soft. Extended drought also causes stems to turn yellow and tough. Ensure your pot receives regular watering without standing in water, and provide light shade during hot summer afternoons.

### When can I move chives outside on my balcony?
Chives are fully cold-hardy perennials, so you do not need to wait for mid-May. You can move outdoor-sown pots or purchased plants outside starting in March. If you started seedlings indoors in warm conditions, harden them off briefly outdoors for a few days before leaving them out permanently.

### How do chives help other plants in small spaces?
Chives are fantastic companion plants. Their strong onion scent confuses pest insects, helping protect nearby crops. Planting chives alongside carrots helps deter carrot rust flies. Grown near fruit trees, chives also help improve resistance against fungal issues. They bring practical benefits to small balcony gardens.

## Benefits
Chives bring a fresh, mild onion flavour to your kitchen. Never cook or heat the stems, or their delicate flavour will disappear. Always add them raw at the very last moment. Chives excel in potato salads, spring omelettes, herb butter, dressings, cold sauces, and creamy quark. They are essential to French fines herbes alongside parsley, tarragon, and chervil. The purple flower petals are fully edible too, offering a colourful, spicy accent for fresh salads.
