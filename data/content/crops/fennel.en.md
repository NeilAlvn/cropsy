## Starting
Knolvenkel loves warmth and steady moisture. For beginners, mid-June to mid-July is the ideal summer sowing window, running up to 20 July. Shortening days after the June solstice help plants focus their energy on swelling crisp bulbs instead of flowers. You can also raise a spring crop indoors from mid-March at 20 °C. Moving young pots outside too early risks cold dips under 10–12 °C, which triggers premature bolting. Summer direct sowing outdoors is easiest. Germination takes roughly 7 to 15 days. Your warm patio or balcony provides a wonderful sheltered spot for starting this rewarding vegetable.

## Seedling
Fennel seedlings develop a sensitive taproot and hate bare-root transplanting. If pre-sowing indoors, choose individual plug trays, soil blocks, or P9 pots with two to three seeds per cell. Once seedlings reach roughly 3 cm, thin them down to a single sturdy plant. Snip excess stems at the soil line instead of pulling them out. This step protects delicate root balls. Move spring seedlings outdoors only after IJsheiligen in mid-May once night frosts pass. Keep the soil ball completely intact.

## Vegetative
Provide each plant with a pot holding at least 10 to 12 litres of airy potting soil mixed with perlite or sharp sand. In square-foot boxes, place one or two plants per 30 by 30 cm square. Give your container full sun or bright, light shade. Keep moisture levels completely steady. Any dry spells cause woody growth and prompt bolting. Apply moderate organic fertilizer or compost, but avoid heavy nitrogen that creates excess leaves. When the swelling base reaches egg size, earth up loose compost around the base to keep it white and shielded.

## Flowering
Drought, root stress, sudden cold, or extreme summer heat can cause bolting. When this happens, stems shoot up to 1.5 metres and bulb growth stops. Keep the plant in your container anyway. Its large yellow umbels attract hoverflies, solitary bees, and parasitic wasps. Fennel foliage also serves as a vital host plant for swallowtail butterfly caterpillars. Later, the flowers produce aromatic, ridged seeds. Let these turn grey-brown on the stems before harvesting them on a dry day.

## Harvest
Harvest your fennel when the bulb feels solid, firm, and reaches the size of a tennis ball or fist. Bulbs should be roughly 8 to 10 cm wide. Cut cleanly through the bulb just above the soil line with a sharp knife. Leave the base and roots undisturbed in the container. Tender mini-fennel shoots frequently sprout from the cut stump for a bonus harvest. Pick autumn crops before persistent freezes, though mature bulbs endure light chills down to -2 °C. Wrapped in a damp cloth, fresh bulbs store well for one to two weeks in the refrigerator.

## FAQ
### Why did my fennel bolt into flower without forming a bulb?
Bolting happens when plants face stress like erratic watering, root disturbance, extreme heat, or cold dips below 10–12 °C. Sowing during the long daylight days of May or June also triggers flowering. To prevent this, sow in July or select bolt-resistant varieties like Fino or Perfektion. Shortening days help bulbs swell.

### Can I grow fennel in a square-foot garden box?
Yes, fennel fits well in square-foot setups. Allocate one to two plants per 30 by 30 cm square. This spacing ensures each bulb receives sufficient sunlight and air circulation. Combine this with moisture-retentive, airy soil and steady hydration to prevent plants from drying out or competing too intensely.

### How do I protect fennel seedlings from slugs?
Damp spring weather makes tender young seedlings a favourite target for garden slugs. You can protect them by elevating containers off the ground on plant stands or patio tables. Alternatively, set up physical barriers or beer traps around your growing area to catch slugs before they reach the foliage.

### What should I do if aphids appear on the stems?
Fennel essential oils deter many pests, but aphids sometimes gather deep between the leaf bases. You can rinse them away easily with a spray of water. Leaving bolted fennel to flower also attracts natural predators like hoverflies and ladybugs, which quickly clean up aphid colonies on your balcony.

### Why is earthing up recommended for fennel bulbs?
Earthing up involves gently mounding 3 to 5 cm of loose compost or potting mix around the base once the bulb reaches egg size. This gardening technique protects the tender bulb from intense midday sunlight and keeps the swollen layers pale, crisp, and beautifully sweet for harvesting.

### When is the safest time to plant spring seedlings outdoors?
Keep indoor spring seedlings sheltered until IJsheiligen in mid-May passes. Early spring weather in the Netherlands brings cold dips below 10–12 °C, which shocks sensitive roots and triggers premature bolting later in summer. Planting out after mid-May ensures warm soil and frost-free nights.

### What type of pot and soil does knolvenkel need?
Use a container holding at least 10 to 12 litres of volume per plant to leave room for the long taproot. Fill it with light, airy compost blended with perlite or sharp sand. The soil must drain freely while holding steady moisture between waterings.

### What wildlife visits fennel plants in the garden?
Fennel flowers provide rich nectar for solitary bees, hoverflies, and parasitic wasps. Additionally, the feathery green foliage is a crucial host plant for caterpillars of the rare swallowtail butterfly in the Low Countries, making it a valuable plant for local balcony biodiversity.

## Benefits
Crisp fennel bulbs offer a delicate, sweet anise flavour. Enjoy them raw by shaving them paper-thin with olive oil, lemon juice, or orange slices. Cooked bulbs turn wonderfully tender when braised in butter, grilled, roasted in the oven, or stirred into risottos and fish soups. Do not discard the dill-like fronds; use them as an aromatic herb for dressings, dips, and fish dishes. Finally, dried seeds make a flavourful addition to marinades, breads, pickling brines, and brewed herbal teas.
