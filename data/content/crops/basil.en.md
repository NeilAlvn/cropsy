## Starting
Start your basil indoors during April or May on a bright windowsill or in a heated propagator. This sun-loving Mediterranean herb craves steady warmth. Seeds sprout quickly in five to ten days when kept between 18 °C and 25 °C, with twenty-two to twenty-five degrees offering the fastest start. Basil seeds need light to emerge. Press them gently into damp seed-starting soil and dust them with a sheer layer of fine compost or vermiculite. Trap moisture by covering your tray with clear glass or plastic wrap. For rainy Dutch balcony conditions, robust Greek bush basil outperforms broadleaf Genovese by handling chilly spells and resisting garden slugs.

## Seedling
Move your seedlings into individual pots once they form two to four true leaves beyond their baby cotyledons. Always hold these delicate starts gently by their leaves, never by their tender stems. Keep them indoors until mid-May passes. Dutch night frosts around IJsheiligen will instantly kill unprotected plants. Harden your basil off over five to seven mild days. Place the pots outside in a sheltered, partially shaded nook during daylight hours, then bring them back inside each night before outdoor temperatures drop.

## Vegetative
Choose deep containers with generous drainage holes and a layer of hydro pebbles to prevent wet roots. Plant single basil plants in pots of at least 1.5 to 3 liters, or set four into a thirty-centimeter square layout. Use a loose mix of sixty percent potting soil and forty percent mature compost. Water at the base or through a bottom saucer every morning. Avoid wetting leaves, and let the surface soil dry slightly between drinks. After four to six weeks, feed plants every two to three weeks with mild organic liquid fertiliser. Stake taller Genovese plants with a thin bamboo stick to withstand gusty balcony winds.

## Flowering
Pinch your basil early to guide its shape. As branches start growing, snip the main center stem directly above two healthy leaves. Two fresh side shoots will emerge from the leaf joints, doubling your stems into a full, bushy crown. Remove emerging flower buds immediately throughout July and August. Letting flowers blossom stops leaf growth and makes foliage tough and bitter. If blooms appear anyway, scatter the mild white or purple petals over your food as a tasty garnish.

## Harvest
Harvest basil by snipping upper shoot tips just above a leaf node where two small leaves are forming. Never strip single bottom leaves, which leaves a bare, exhausted stem. Constant top snipping encourages vigorous summer regrowth. Pick your stems on a dry, sunny morning right after dew evaporates, when aromatic essential oils are peak and before midday heat weakens them. Never dry your basil leaves, as fragile aromatics vanish completely. Instead, freeze chopped leaves in ice cube trays submerged in water or oil, or blend them smoothly with olive oil. You can also steep fresh sprigs directly into quality vinegar or neutral oil.

## FAQ
### How can I revive a potted supermarket basil plant?
Supermarket pots contain dozens of crowded seedlings with cramped roots. Gently slide the root ball out and tease it apart into three or four separate clusters. Repot each cluster into a roomy container with fresh potting soil. Water thoroughly from beneath and place on a warm, bright windowsill, sheltered from direct scorching sun for several days.

### How do I stop slugs from eating my basil?
Slugs devour soft basil leaves overnight. Keep your containers off garden soil by placing them on elevated balcony tables, raised planters, or plant stands. You can also install physical barriers or beer traps around your pots. Alternatively, choose small-leaved Greek bush basil, which naturally deters slugs due to its denser, firmer foliage.

### Why are my basil leaves turning yellow?
Yellowing lower leaves point to three common issues in Dutch patio growing: waterlogged potting soil that suffocates roots, cold roots when temperatures drop below 12 °C to 15 °C, or depleted nitrogen in container soil. Let the soil surface dry slightly, protect pots from cold snaps, and provide mild organic liquid feed.

### Why do young basil seedlings suddenly collapse?
Seedlings collapse from fungal damping-off when kept too wet in cold rooms with stagnant air. Protect young plants by providing bright light, proper ventilation, and warm room conditions. Always water seedlings gently from below via a bottom tray rather than pouring water over the delicate stems and leaves from above.

### When is it safe to put basil outside?
Never leave basil outside before mid-May, once the Dutch IJsheiligen frost period has passed. Late cold snaps or brief near-freezing nights will quickly stunt or kill the plant. Harden plants off over five to seven mild days by moving them outside during daytime and indoors every night.

### What basil variety grows best in the Dutch climate?
Classic Italian Genovese basil is popular, but compact Greek bush basil performs significantly better outdoors in the Netherlands. Its smaller, tougher leaves endure fluctuating maritime temperatures and windy, rainy weather far better. Bush basil also naturally resists damage from balcony pests like slugs much better than broadleaf varieties.

### What is the best way to water container basil?
Basil roots require steady moisture but rot quickly in soggy soil. Pour water directly at the base of the plant or supply it through a bottom reservoir tray, ideally in the morning. Keep water completely off the foliage to avoid fungal disease, and let topsoil dry slightly before watering again.

### Can I dry my harvested basil leaves?
Avoid drying basil leaves, as the delicate aromatic oils quickly vanish during the drying process. Preserve your harvest by freezing instead. Chop the leaves and freeze them in ice cube trays topped with water or oil, or blend the fresh leaves with olive oil. You can also infuse fresh stems into vinegar.

## Benefits
Basil brings vibrant summer brightness to your kitchen. Always add the leaves fresh at the very end of cooking, because high heat quickly destroys their aromatic essential oils. Fresh leaves shine in homemade Italian pesto, Caprese tomato salads, warm pasta dishes, and comforting soups. For creative summer treats, blend fresh basil into chilled strawberry soup, stir chopped leaves through fruit yoghurt, or whip them into homemade compound herb butters and herb vinegars to enhance your cooking.
