## Starting
Start seeds indoors from mid-March to April in individual pots. Keep them in a bright, cool room around fifteen degrees Celsius. Warm windowsills make young shoots weak and leggy. You can also sow directly outside into balcony containers from mid-April through May. Dutch growers strongly advise waiting until after Ice Saints in mid-May for outdoor sowing. The seeds and tender young sprouts cannot tolerate frost. Press the large seeds down into the soil. At cool spring temperatures, germination usually takes between seven and fifteen days. Keep the compost lightly moist as you wait for the green shoots to emerge.

## Seedling
Nasturtium seedlings have delicate root systems that resent disturbance. Avoid traditional pricking out entirely. Raising them in individual paper rolls or biodegradable pots protects the fragile roots when moving. If several sprouts emerge together, thin them down to leave only the strongest seedling. Move your young plants outdoors strictly after Ice Saints have passed in mid-May. Once the risk of freezing nights is gone, your seedlings will settle into containers happily and grow rapidly.

## Vegetative
Choose containers with drainage holes to avoid soggy compost. Compact varieties fit well in balcony boxes or pots holding five to seven liters. Trailing types produce long runners and thrive best in pots holding ten to fifteen liters. Position them in sun or light shade. Sunlight encourages heavy flowering, while excessive shade produces lush greenery without blooms. Keep the soil moderately moist, as plants handle brief dry spells much better than standing water. Crucially, avoid nitrogen fertilizers. Poor, lean soil triggers abundant flowering. Rich compost or heavy feeding makes the plant grow massive leafy cushions while suppressing flower buds completely.

## Flowering
Flowering starts in June or July and continues until the first autumn frost arrives. Blooms appear in cheerful yellow, orange, red, and salmon-pink tones. These nectar-filled blossoms are great favorites of visiting bees and bumblebees. Regularly snip off faded flowers to maintain a continuous display. If you let the plant form seed pods, it directs its energy into fruit ripening and stops creating fresh buds. Consistent deadheading keeps balcony containers colorful for months.

## Harvest
Harvest fresh leaves continually while they are young and tender. Older foliage turns tough with an overly sharp bite. Pick flowers in the morning right after opening. Use them immediately, or store them in the refrigerator inside an airtight box with a damp paper towel for one or two days. Plump, green seed pods can be gathered while firm and juicy. Soak them in salted water, then preserve them in spiced vinegar with dill, peppercorns, and garlic as faux capers. In autumn, let select seed pods turn brown and drop naturally. Collect, dry them inside on a dish, and store them in paper bags for spring.

## FAQ
### Why are my nasturtium leaves turning yellow?
Yellow leaves usually signal poor pot drainage or overly wet soil, as roots hate sitting in water. Ensure your container has open drainage holes. Yellowing lower foliage can also indicate natural aging in dense clumps or a slight lack of nitrogen in worn-out container soil.

### Why does the plant have plenty of leaves but no flowers?
Lush foliage paired with zero flower buds is almost always caused by over-fertilizing with nitrogen or planting in a spot that is too shady. Nasturtium thrives in lean, poor soil and full sunlight. Stop all feeding immediately and shift the pot to a sunnier balcony position.

### How does nasturtium work as a trap crop?
Mustard oils in the plant attract black bean aphids and cabbage white caterpillars away from vegetables like cabbage and beans. By sacrificing a nasturtium plant as a decoy, you keep pests off main crops while drawing beneficial predators like ladybug larvae and hoverflies to your balcony.

### Will my nasturtium survive the Dutch winter?
No, nasturtium cannot survive freezing conditions. At the very first frost below zero degrees Celsius, the foliage turns to mush and the plant dies. However, plants frequently drop viable seeds in late summer, which can sprout spontaneously in container compost the following spring.

### Why are my indoor seedlings becoming weak and leggy?
Seedlings stretch into spindly, pale stems when started in conditions that are too warm, especially on heated indoor windowsills. Grow them in a bright location kept around fifteen degrees Celsius or inside a cold greenhouse. Cool air and bright light produce strong, compact growth.

### When is it safe to put plants outdoors?
Both seeds and seedlings are strictly frost-tender and cannot cope with freezing temperatures. Dutch growers advise planting outdoors or moving seedling pots out only after Ice Saints in mid-May. Once the threat of frost is behind you, your plants will flourish safely outside.

### How do I support trailing nasturtiums on a balcony?
Climbing varieties have flexible leaf stems that naturally coil around trellises, bamboo canes, or mesh. You can guide these long runners upward to create a leafy green screen, or simply let them tumble over the edges of high pots, railing boxes, or raised garden beds.

### How do I save nasturtium seeds for next year?
Let several green fruit clusters mature on the vines toward the end of summer. Wait until the seed pods turn completely brown and drop off the stems naturally. Collect them, let them dry thoroughly indoors on a small dish, and store them in paper envelopes.

## Benefits
Nasturtium offers a sharp, peppery taste very similar to fresh watercress or radish. The bright flowers make a gorgeous edible garnish scattered over summer salads, cold pasta, or savory cakes. You can also stuff whole blooms with soft herb cheese. Chop young leaves finely into garden salads, blend them into bold pestos, or mix them into homemade herb butter. Stir tender foliage into warm potato mash or fresh soup right at the end of cooking.
