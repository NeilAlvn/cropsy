## Starting
Start your courgette indoors from mid-April through May. These plants love warmth and hate frost. Keep your pots between 20 °C and 25 °C for quick germination within four to ten days. You can pre-germinate seeds between damp kitchen paper or place seeds on their edge in moist seed compost to prevent rot. Move young plants outside only after IJsheiligen in mid-May once night frosts finish and balcony soil warms up. If you miss the spring window, you can continue sowing until early July. This provides a fresh harvest throughout late summer and autumn.

## Seedling
Sow one seed per pot to protect the fragile root system. As soon as the two seed lobes open, move the pot to your brightest windowsill or cold frame. Lower the ambient temperature to around 16 °C to 18 °C. Too much heat with weak indoor light makes seedlings stretch into weak, leggy stems. In early to mid-May, harden off your seedlings over a full week before placing them outdoors permanently.

## Vegetative
Plant in containers holding at least 10 to 20 litres with drainage holes. Courgettes drink heavily because their broad leaves transpire fast. Water steadily at the base so the pot never dries completely. Shape an earthen ring in the soil to funnel water straight to roots. These plants are greedy feeders. Mix compost into the pot initially and feed organic vegetable fertilizer every two to three weeks. If space is tight, pick a climbing variety like Black Forest F1. Tie the main vine weekly to a sturdy bamboo frame up to two metres high. Guiding early avoids snapped stems.

## Flowering
Courgettes produce distinct male and female blooms. Slender male stalks emerge first to call pollinators. Female blossoms have a noticeable swollen baby fruit directly behind the petals. Bees handle pollination outdoors, but enclosed balconies need your help. Simply snap off a fresh male flower and peel back the petals. Gently brush the pollen-covered stamen right into the centre of an open female bloom. This easy hand pollinating guarantees fruit set.

## Harvest
Pick courgettes when they reach 15 to 20 cm in length, or 7 to 10 cm for round cultivars. At this size, the seeds stay small and skins remain tender. Check your plants every two or three days during warm summer stretches. Fast harvests encourage the plant to churn out flowers, yielding up to thirty fruits per plant. Always cut the fruit clean with a sharp knife, leaving a short stem. Store unwashed courgettes in a pantry or cellar at 8 °C to 12 °C. Never store them below 5 °C in cold fridges to prevent decay, and keep them away from apples or tomatoes.

## FAQ
### Why are my small courgettes rotting at the tips?
Young fruits rot from the flower end when pollination is incomplete or during persistent rainy spells. Without enough bee visits, the unfertilised embryo aborts. Hand pollinate blossoms on damp mornings to secure growth. When plants sit in garden beds, tuck clean straw beneath developing fruits to keep them off wet mud.

### How can I treat powdery mildew on the leaves?
Powdery mildew appears late in summer as white dust on older foliage. It is a natural aging issue brought on by humid nights. Prune out heavily coated leaves to improve air movement. You can spray a mix of 40% milk and 60% water over leaves in full sunshine.

### Why are the leaves on my courgette turning yellow?
Yellow foliage usually points to three common issues in containers. Roots often turn pale if soil is too cold early in the season. Poor drainage and standing water also drown roots. Finally, courgettes are heavy feeders, so yellowing can indicate a lack of nitrogen requiring organic vegetable fertilizer.

### How do I grow courgettes on a small balcony?
Choose a climbing type such as Black Forest F1 in a pot holding at least 10 to 20 litres. Provide bamboo canes or a trellis up to two metres tall. Tie the vine loosely every week. Vertical growth saves floor space and keeps leaves dry and healthy.

### Are courgette flowers edible?
Yes, both male and female blooms make delicious treats. Harvest male flowers on long stems, or female blooms attached to baby courgettes. Fill them with ricotta and herbs, dip them in a light batter or beaten egg, and fry until crisp. Pick flowers early in the morning when fresh.

### What can I do with oversized courgettes?
Large marrows develop tougher skin and bigger seeds, but you should not waste them. Grate the flesh into puréed soups or quick bakes. You can also slice the marrow and pickle it in sweet-and-sour brine with vinegar, mustard seeds, and turmeric for long-lasting winter jars.

### Is it dangerous if a homegrown courgette tastes bitter?
Never eat bitter courgettes. Bitterness indicates toxic cucurbitacins, which can occur if plants cross-pollinate with ornamental gourds. Cooking does not destroy these compounds, and ingesting them causes severe stomach cramps and nausea. Always taste a raw sliver first, and spit out and discard any fruit that tastes bitter.

### How often should I water and feed container courgettes?
Potted courgettes dry out and lose nutrients quickly. Water regularly at the base so the potting soil stays moist without splashing foliage. Enrich the container with compost before planting. Then apply liquid organic vegetable fertilizer every two to three weeks throughout the summer to support continuous flowering.

## Benefits
Fresh courgettes offer incredible flexibility in the kitchen. Roast tender slices with herbs, toss them into quick stir-fries, or simmer them into ratatouille and vegetable curries. Their mild flavour also shines when blended into creamy, puréed soups. Don't forget the golden flowers, which fry beautifully when stuffed with cheese. If any fruits hide and grow oversized, turn them into sweet-and-sour pickles with vinegar, mustard seeds, and turmeric to enjoy home-grown garden flavour throughout the colder months.
