## Starting
Runner beans are sensitive to frost. Cold, wet spring soil can rot seeds or attract mice and birds. Start seeds indoors on a bright windowsill or greenhouse from late April to early May, about two to three weeks before planting out. Pre-soaking large seeds in tepid water for up to twenty-four hours speeds up sprouting. If sowing directly outdoors on your balcony, wait until after IJsheiligen in mid-May when night frosts pass. Outdoor soil temperatures should reach 12 to 15 °C for reliable growth. Sow seeds two to three centimeters deep. Outdoor sowings can continue through late June.

## Seedling
These plants hate root disturbance and develop fragile taproots, so never prick them out. Sow directly into P9 pots, cardboard toilet rolls, or root trainers. This keeps the root plug completely intact when transplanting. Acclimate your seedlings to the balcony after IJsheiligen through gradual hardening off. Set them in a sheltered spot away from intense midday sun for one hour. Slowly increase their outdoor time over five to seven days before leaving them outside overnight.

## Vegetative
Choose a container of at least twenty to thirty liters or a mortar tub with drainage holes for three to four plants. Weight the container base with ballast so summer winds do not tip it. Build a wigwam using three or four bamboo canes or willow branches tied at the top. Push the canes into the pot edges and plant two to three beans per cane. Pronkbonen tolerate cool, windy weather well but require plenty of water. Keep the container soil consistently moist and add a layer of compost or grass clipping mulch. Avoid high-nitrogen feeds to prevent excessive leafy growth; choose compost and potassium-rich fertilizer.

## Flowering
Clusters of red, white, or bicolored blooms appear from late June until autumn frost. Runner beans require insect cross-pollination to set pods, depending heavily on wild bumblebees. Hot spells above 28 to 30 °C or dry air can cause blossom drop when pollen dries out. Support your plants by keeping the potting soil damp during flowering. On very hot summer days, gently mist the leaves and blossoms with cool water to help stabilize fruit set.

## Harvest
Harvest pods when they are young, slender, and tender. Pick every two to three days to stimulate new flower formation. Although pods can reach twenty-five to thirty centimeters, older pods become fibrous and develop tough, leathery walls. If pods mature past the tender stage, leave them on the vine until early autumn turns them brown and brittle. Shell out the patterned purple-black or white dry beans and store them in airtight glass jars. For freezing fresh pods, slice them, blanch in boiling water for two minutes, and plunge into ice water. You can also preserve pods by traditional salting in crocks or fermenting.

## FAQ
### Why are my bean blossoms dropping without forming pods?
High temperatures above 28 to 30 °C dry out pollen, preventing fruit set. Dry air and under-watered soil also trigger blossom drop. Keep the container soil consistently moist when flower buds appear. On hot summer days, gently mist the foliage and flowers with cool water to keep them refreshed and encourage bumblebee pollination.

### How do I protect young seedlings from slugs and snails?
Snails and slugs love eating tender young runner bean shoots in spring. To protect them, place protective collars, such as cut plastic bottles or bottomless pots, over young plants. Alternatively, keep seedlings growing in small pots indoors until they are fifteen centimeters tall before moving them out to the balcony.

### Can I prick out runner bean seedlings into larger pots?
No, avoid pricking them out. Runner beans form delicate taproots that dislike any root disturbance. Dutch gardening guides recommend sowing single seeds directly into individual P9 pots, cardboard toilet rolls, or root trainers. When planting time arrives, you can place the intact root plug directly into the final container without causing shock.

### What should I do if black bean aphids attack my plants?
Runner beans resist aphids better than broad beans, but colonies of black bean aphids can still cluster on young shoot tips. You can control them easily without chemicals. Wash the pests off with a firm jet of water, or pinch out heavily infested growing tips to keep the rest of your vine clean.

### Can I eat runner beans raw from the vine?
Never eat raw runner bean pods or dry beans. They contain phasin, a natural plant lectin that causes stomach cramps and digestive irritation. Thorough boiling, steaming, or cooking fully breaks down phasin, making the beans completely safe to enjoy. However, you can eat the colorful flowers raw in fresh summer salads.

### How do I stop tall bean tipis from blowing over in balcony winds?
Runner beans climb vigorously up to three meters and handle gusty winds well, but containers can tip over. Use a large, heavy container or mortar tub of at least twenty to thirty liters. Add ballast to the bottom and tie the bamboo canes securely to a balcony railing or sturdy wall.

### Do runner beans need wild bees to set pods?
Yes, unlike common green beans that pollinate themselves, runner beans depend heavily on insect cross-pollination. Their large blossoms require wild pollinators, especially bumblebees, to trip the pollination mechanism. Without visiting bumblebees, the flowers will not develop into pods. Growing them in an open balcony spot ensures visiting pollinators find them.

### Which flower colors should I choose for warm balconies?
Runner beans come in bright red, pure white, and bicolored varieties. While red varieties like 'Preisgewinner' look stunning, white-flowering varieties like 'Emergo' often set fruit more reliably during hot summer weather. If your balcony gets very warm, white-flowering cultivars help prevent poor pollination and dropped flowers during heatwaves.

## Benefits
Runner beans offer versatile kitchen uses throughout the growing season. Enjoy the blossoms raw as a salad garnish with their delicate, sweet nectar flavor. Sliced young pods resemble traditional Dutch snijbonen. Sauté or boil them with summer savory, butter, or garlic after harvesting. Fully mature shelled beans make wonderful winter staples. Soak dried purple-black or white beans overnight and simmer them into hearty stews, soups, or marinated bean salads. Always cook pods and beans thoroughly to break down phasin lectins.
