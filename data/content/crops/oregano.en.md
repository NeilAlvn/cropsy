## Starting
Start your oregano journey indoors during March or April. These tiny seeds love warmth and sprout within one to two weeks at room temperature. Always remember that oregano seeds need light to wake up. Never bury them under soil. Simply press them gently onto the surface of your compost. If you prefer, scatter a whisper-thin layer of vermiculite or coarse sand over them. Mix light seed compost with about twenty percent coarse sand for healthy aeration. This gritty blend prevents damping-off and keeps roots happy. You can also sow seeds directly into outdoor pots from mid-April into May.

## Seedling
Sow three to five seeds in small pots. Once the young plants grow their first true leaves, prick them out or thin them down. Spring weather can be fickle on a Dutch balcony. Tender seedlings dislike sudden cold snaps and temperature swings in late April. Keep them protected inside or sheltered. Wait patiently until mid-May, after the cold threat of IJsheiligen passes, before moving them permanently into outdoor containers or garden beds.

## Vegetative
Choose a generous pot with drainage holes so excess water drains freely. Oregano loves well-draining, neutral or chalky soil with modest nutrients. Heavy clay soil must be loosened with grit, sand, or compost. Never over-fertilise your plant. Too much plant food encourages soft growth and robs the leaves of their pungent essential oils. Oregano handles dry spells easily once rooted. Let the top layers of potting mix dry out between waterings, as soggy soil leads to root rot. No stakes are needed. The herb naturally grows into a neat, woody-stemmed shrublet up to sixty centimetres high.

## Flowering
Between July and September, your oregano bursts into pinkish-purple blossoms. These cheerful flowers transform your balcony into a bustling haven for bumblebees, honeybees, and small copper butterflies. The delicate blossoms are fully edible, offering a sweet, mild herbal note. Sprinkle them over fresh salads for a lovely splash of colour. In autumn, leave the fading seed heads standing on your plant. Hungry garden birds will happily visit your winter containers to feast on the mature seeds.

## Harvest
Snip leaves and stem tips all summer long whenever you need fresh flavour. For the richest aroma, harvest on a dry, sunny morning just before or during flowering. Oregano preserves wonderfully. Bundle the stems loosely and hang them upside down in a warm, dark, breezy room for about three weeks. You can also spread single leaves across a clean cloth to dry. Once crisp, strip the leaves into an airtight jar for up to a year. Alternatively, chop the fresh leaves and freeze them in ice cube trays with olive oil or water.

## FAQ
### Can oregano survive the winter on a Dutch balcony?
Yes, oregano is fully winter-hardy in the Low Countries, though it dies back above ground. The biggest winter threat is cold, soggy soil rather than freezing temperatures. Keep your container safe from root rot by placing it on pot feet or moving it into a sheltered spot away from constant winter rain.

### When and how should I prune my oregano plant?
Give your oregano a thorough tidy in early spring, around April or May, once hard frosts have passed. Cut the dead, brown stems back to just above the soil level. This clean sweep encourages strong, compact new growth and refreshes the woody base for the coming summer season.

### Will oregano spread and take over my container garden?
Oregano spreads outward via underground rhizomes and can quickly crowd neighbouring plants in a small garden box. Growing it in its own dedicated pot keeps it contained. If you plant it in shared soil, simply sever the creeping root runners every spring to keep the plant within bounds.

### Do slugs and snails attack oregano?
Slugs and snails strongly dislike oregano because of its potent, fragrant essential oils. In fact, gardeners often use it as a natural slug barrier. You can grow it in your balcony pots or near vulnerable crops without worrying about snails destroying the foliage.

### What pests should I watch out for during warm weather?
While snails stay away, containers placed against warm, sunny brick walls can attract spider mites or thrips during hot summer stretches. Keep an eye on the leaves during prolonged dry spells. Giving your plants good air circulation helps prevent these tiny pests from establishing themselves on your balcony.

### How often should I water my oregano container?
Water sparingly. Oregano loves dry conditions once it settles in. Always check the soil before reaching for your watering can, and let the top layer dry out completely between waterings. Soggy roots will rot quickly, so always ensure excess water drains out freely from the bottom holes.

### Why does my potted oregano have weak flavour?
Weak flavour usually stems from too much fertiliser. Oregano thrives in light to moderate fertility. Over-feeding stimulates lush, soft foliage, but it dilutes the aromatic oils that give the herb its punchy taste. Skip heavy feeding to enjoy leaves packed with deep, concentrated herbal scent.

### What should I do when my plant outgrows its pot?
Container-grown perennial herbs will eventually circle their roots around the pot walls over time. When growth slows down, repot into a slightly roomier container with fresh, gritty mix. Alternatively, lift the plant and prune the root ball every few seasons to keep your shrublet vigorous and healthy.

## Benefits
Oregano brings classic Mediterranean warmth to your kitchen. It is wonderful in tomato sauces, pizza toppings, roasted potatoes, bean stews, and roasted meats. Because the fresh leaves contain delicate aromatic oils, stir them in right at the end of cooking to keep their bright flavour intact. Dried oregano holds up well to gentle simmering. You can also steep fresh leaves in cold-pressed olive oil alongside garlic and chili, or blend them with coarse sea salt for fragrant homemade seasoning.
