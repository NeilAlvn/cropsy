## Starting
Growing onions in pots or balcony boxes is easy and rewarding. Onion sets are ideal for compact containers. They grow quickly, stay sturdy, and resist pests better than seeds. Plant spring sets from March through mid-April, as they handle light spring frost with ease. For an early harvest next year, tuck winter sets into soil between mid-September and mid-November. If you prefer growing from seeds, start them indoors in late winter or direct-sow outside during early spring. Simply press your onion sets gently into potting soil until the pointed tips poke out at the surface.

## Seedling
Using sets makes life simple because you never need to prick out or thin seedlings. If starting from seeds, sprouts emerge within two to four weeks. Thin them out once tiny green blades rise a few centimeters. Handle young seedlings gently to avoid damaging fine, delicate roots. Keep their root clumps intact during any necessary transplanting. Because onion starts are naturally cold-hardy, hardened plants can stay out on your balcony well before mid-May without fear of frost.

## Vegetative
Choose pots at least fifteen to twenty centimeters deep with clear drainage holes. Place containers in full sun where leaves catch six hours of light. In square planters, tuck nine to sixteen sets into a single section. Water moderately while leaves grow and bulbs swell, letting moisture ease off as bulbs finish ripening. Sogginess causes bulb rot. Avoid excess nitrogen or fresh manure, as heavy feeding creates soft, leafy bulbs. Instead, offer light potassium-rich organic feed for firm bulbs. Provide a wind-sheltered spot on breezy balconies to protect taller foliage.

## Flowering
Onions are biennial plants that naturally set seed in their second year. Early heat, sudden cold snaps, or dry soil can trigger bolting prematurely. Once a flower stem rises, the bulb stops swelling and turns woody inside. Snip off budding flower stalks right away to slow this down, or pull the onion for fresh kitchen use. Left intact, round onion blooms produce rich nectar that attracts busy bees and bumblebees to your balcony garden.

## Harvest
Harvest winter sets between late spring and midsummer, while spring varieties finish during July or August. Watch for the leaves to turn yellow and flop over entirely. Leave the bulbs resting in their pots for another week or two before pulling them up completely by hand. Dry the freshly pulled bulbs for up to two weeks under a dry porch roof or on dry ground. Once outer skins are crisp and papery, pack them into crates or hang them in net bags in a cool, dark, frost-free space.

## FAQ
### Should I grow onions from seeds or sets?
For small gardens and containers, sets are the best choice. They establish quickly, require no tricky thinning, and handle weather swings nicely. Sets are also naturally more resistant to the onion fly. Growing from seed is possible, but seedlings take weeks to emerge and need careful thinning and delicate root handling.

### What size pot do I need for onions?
Select containers or troughs that have a depth of at least fifteen to twenty centimeters. Always verify that your pots have drainage holes at the bottom so water flows away easily. In standard square meter boxes, you can comfortably fit nine to sixteen sets inside a single grid square.

### Can onions stay outside during cold weather?
Yes, onions are naturally cold-hardy crops. Spring sets easily endure light frosts outdoors. Hardened seedlings can remain on balconies well before mid-May without issues. Additionally, hardy winter varieties planted between mid-September and mid-November withstand chilly conditions throughout winter, providing a very early harvest the next year.

### How should I water my container onions?
Keep the potting soil moderately moist while leaves and bulbs are growing actively. Never allow the container soil to become waterlogged, because wet soil quickly triggers root and bulb rot. As the onions reach full size and begin to mature, reduce watering and let the potting mix dry out gradually.

### What fertilizer is best for growing onions?
Onions thrive on moderate feeding. Avoid heavy nitrogen fertilizers or fresh manure, because high nitrogen leads to floppy foliage and soft bulbs that spoil fast in storage. Instead, apply a modest amount of potassium-rich organic fertilizer to promote firm, healthy bulbs that store well through the colder months.

### Why did my onion plant form a flower stalk?
Temperature swings, late cold waves, early summer heat, or dry soil stress cause onions to bolt. When a flower stem appears, the bulb stops growing and turns tough inside. Cut off the young stem immediately to salvage the bulb, or harvest and eat the entire onion fresh right away.

### How do I protect onions from pests and birds?
Cover containers with fine insect netting having a zero-point-eight millimeter mesh to keep onion flies and leek miners away. Interplanting with carrots also confuses the flies. Newly planted sets often look like worms to curious birds, so lay temporary netting across pots during the first few weeks after planting.

### Why is crop rotation necessary for alliums?
Avoid growing onions in the same pot or plot in consecutive years. Wait four to five years before planting any members of the allium family, including leeks and shallots, in that exact soil. This rest period prevents stubborn fungal diseases like downy mildew and keeps your soil fresh.

### How do I properly dry and store my onions?
After pulling bulbs when tops collapse, let them cure for one to two weeks under an airy shelter or on dry ground. Once skins are completely dry, store them in crates, breathable net bags, or braided ropes in a cool, dark, frost-free, well-ventilated area. Yellow onions store longest.

## Benefits
Onions form the essential base for daily home cooking. Mild, sweet red varieties shine raw in crisp salads, while pungent yellow bulbs are ideal for slow stews and baking. Snip tender green tops from winter onions to use like scallions or chives. Even bolted plants are tasty; stir-fry the young flower stems and small bulbs for a delicious, sweet meal. Pluck pretty round blossom florets to garnish your favorite savory balcony dinners.
