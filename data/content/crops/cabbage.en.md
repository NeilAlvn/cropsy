## Starting
Start seeds indoors or under glass from February to early April at 18 to 20 °C for summer cabbages. Autumn and winter storage types follow from April to early May. Warm conditions above 15 °C guarantee rapid germination. You can also explore traditional Dutch weeuwenteelt by sowing hardy types in autumn cold frames to overwinter. Always start in modular trays instead of direct sowing outdoors. Seedling trays keep young sprouts safe from hungry birds, slugs, and destructive cabbage flies. Your balcony will soon have sturdy green starters ready for potting up once true leaves show.

## Seedling
Prick out seedlings into individual pots when their first true leaves appear. Plant each baby stem deeply right up to its lowest leaves. This encourages adventitious roots and anchors plants against windy weather. Harden them off outdoors over seven to ten days before moving outside permanently. Wait until late April through early June to transplant into your garden or balcony tubs. Planting after mid-May mid-spring cold snaps lets roots establish rapidly in warm soil.

## Vegetative
White cabbages are expansive, heavy feeders that demand ample space. Choose a sturdy pot of at least 25 to 40 liters with generous drainage holes, or dedicate four garden squares. Fill containers with humus-rich, firm soil enriched with mature compost and organic manure pellets. Mix in lime if using acidic sand to lift pH. Heel the soil down firmly around the base to anchor the heavy head securely against the wind. Water deeply at the soil surface, never splashing foliage, to protect the roots. A thick mulch of straw or compost keeps container moisture stable all season.

## Flowering
White cabbage is a biennial plant. It focuses on growing a tight, leafy head during its initial season. Exposure to severe cold snaps, drought, or root disturbance can cause premature bolting. Stressed plants bypass head formation, sending up bitter, woody yellow flowers instead. True flowering and seed pods naturally arrive in the second year following winter vernalization. For culinary use, harvest your cabbage promptly in year one before any flower spikes emerge.

## Harvest
Check your cabbage by pressing the center firmly with your hand. Harvest only when the heart feels completely solid and rock-hard. Storage cabbages must be gathered in late autumn before hard frosts strike, as internal freezing ruins stored heads. Slice the head clean from the root stem using a sharp knife, keeping several protective wrapper leaves intact. Whole heads keep for three to four months in a cool, dark, frost-free shed or cellar. You can also shred and blanch the leaves for two minutes to freeze them for up to twelve months, or ferment them.

## FAQ
### How do I stop cabbage white caterpillars from eating my leaves?
Both large and small cabbage white butterflies lay eggs under the foliage, producing hungry caterpillars that can defoliate your crop. Cover your cabbage immediately after planting out with fine insect netting having a mesh size of one millimeter or smaller. Keep it sealed around pot rims.

### How can I protect young plants from cabbage root flies?
Cabbage root flies lay eggs at the soil line, where maggots feast on young roots. Fit a flat collar around the stem base at ground level. Alternatively, use the Velt method of planting the stem horizontally beneath the soil to block flies from reaching the root neck.

### What causes cabbage heads to suddenly split open?
Mature heads often burst following heavy rain after a dry spell. The roots abruptly surge excess water into an already rigid head. To stop this, twist the mature head ninety degrees with your hands or sever half the root system with a spade to throttle water uptake.

### How do I manage clubroot in my garden soil?
Clubroot is a persistent soil-borne organism that deforms roots and stunts growth. To prevent it, enforce a strict six-year crop rotation for all brassicas. Maintain sweet, alkaline soil by adding seaweed lime to keep the soil pH above 6.5 to 7.0, which discourages the organism.

### Do I need protection against birds on a balcony?
Yes, urban wood pigeons relish tender cabbage leaves and can strip young transplants quickly. Drape light bird netting over your containers during the initial weeks. Once the plant develops broad, tough foliage and strong stems, the netting can be removed safely.

### Can I grow white cabbage in a standard square foot garden?
Standard closed-head cabbage grows too broad for a single thirty-centimeter square. It easily overtakes neighboring plants. You must allocate four joined squares to give the expansive leaf canopy enough room, or simply grow each cabbage in its own dedicated, deep patio tub.

### What is the traditional Dutch weeuwenteelt method?
Weeuwenteelt is an old winter technique where winter-hardy cabbage varieties are sown during September or October. You shelter the young seedlings in a cold frame through the cold months. They overwinter as compact plants, granting an extra-early, rewarding harvest when spring arrives.

### Why must I plant the seedlings so deeply?
Sinking seedlings down to their lowest leaves stimulates adventitious root growth along the buried stem. This establishes a much stronger root base to nourish this hungry crop. Deep planting also prevents leggy stems and securely anchors top-heavy heads against gusty coastal winds.

## Benefits
Crunchy raw leaves make crisp slaws with grated apple, walnuts, and cider vinegar. For traditional sauerkraut, shred leaves finely, massage with salt, and ferment for several weeks into tangy kraut. Slice whole heads into thick slabs to roast cabbage steaks with olive oil, garlic, and cumin in a hot oven. Firm leaves also hold up wonderfully in slow winter braises, rustic vegetable soups, curries, and fast stir-fries.
