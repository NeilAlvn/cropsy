## Starting
Start sage seeds indoors from late March into April. Keep seed trays at warm room temperatures around 18–20 °C. Cover the seeds very lightly with fine compost or coarse sand. Germination typically takes two to three weeks. You can also start plants from semi-ripe heel or stem cuttings in July or August, which root easily in sandy soil. Young sage plants are sensitive to ground frosts. Keep them protected inside during early spring. Wait until the cold risk passes after IJsheiligen in mid-May before setting your plants outside on a sunny balcony or garden terrace.

## Seedling
Prick out seedlings once they grow two to three pairs of true leaves. Move them gently into individual small nursery pots. Always lift little plants by their leaves, never by their delicate stems. Place them in a bright, sunny windowsill or an unheated greenhouse. Before moving them outdoors permanently after IJsheiligen, harden off your seedlings over seven to ten days. Set them out during warm afternoons, then bring them back inside for chilly nights.

## Vegetative
Choose a generous container with drainage holes and add hydro granules or potsherds at the base. Sage craves lean, airy soil. Mix regular potting soil with coarse river sand, fine gravel, or lava grit. Never use rich compost or heavy peat. Let the topsoil dry out between waterings. Soggy conditions and stagnant water quickly rot the roots. Feed very sparingly. High nitrogen causes weak growth and ruins the essential oils. A light sprinkle of organic herb fertilizer in late spring is plenty. Strong square stems form a sturdy bush, so your plant needs no stakes in full sun.

## Flowering
Spikes of violet, blue, or purple flowers open during June and July. These nectar-rich blooms draw bumblebees, honeybees, hoverflies, and butterflies to your balcony. The two-lipped petals are edible and carry a delicate, sweet flavor. As blooms fade, the plant produces four-parted nutlets. If you want dense leaf growth, snip off the spent flowering stems immediately. This keeps the plant from wasting energy on seeds and encourages fresh, compact shoots to sprout.

## Harvest
You can pick fresh leaves and shoot tips right through the year. Peak flavor and aromatic oils occur in May and June, just before flower buds open. Sage leaves dry beautifully. Hang loose bunches upside down in a warm, well-ventilated, shaded spot. With proper airflow, leaves turn crisp within a week. Store dry leaves in airtight glass jars out of direct light. Alternatively, freeze chopped leaves in ice cube trays with water or olive oil. You can also steep whole leaves in vinegar or coarse salt to preserve their aroma for later use.

## FAQ
### How do I protect potted sage during Dutch winters?
Potted roots freeze much faster than plants in garden soil. Wet roots combined with freezing temperatures can quickly kill sage. During severe cold snaps, shift your containers against a sheltered, south-facing house wall. You can also store pots inside an unheated shed or cold frame until hard frosts pass.

### How should I prune an older woody plant?
Prune in spring once severe frosts pass, around April or early May. Always cut back just above fresh, healthy green shoots. Never cut deeply into old, bare wood. Lignified woody stems rarely break dormant buds, and deep cutting can kill the entire branch.

### What should I do about the rosemary beetle?
The rosemary beetle is a metallic green and purple striped beetle. Both the adults and their larvae feed on shoot tips in autumn and spring. Inspect your plants regularly. Hand-pick the beetles and larvae off your sage bushes to keep damage low without using chemicals.

### Do slugs damage sage plants on balconies?
Slugs rarely eat mature sage leaves. The foliage contains pungent essential oils that naturally deter snails and slugs. Because of this strong aroma, sage makes a great companion plant. You can position containers near more vulnerable greens to help create a natural barrier against slugs.

### How do I prevent powdery mildew on my sage?
Powdery mildew appears as white coating on foliage, triggered by stagnant, damp air around dense leaves. Always place your pots in open, breezy areas where sunlight reaches the stems. Keep pots elevated off wet ground and maintain good airflow between your balcony containers.

### What kind of soil mix works best in containers?
Sage needs very lean, well-draining soil. Standard potting compost holds far too much moisture. Mix potting soil with coarse river sand, lava grit, or fine gravel. Add hydro granules or potsherds to the container base, and avoid adding rich compost or peat.

### How often should I water my potted sage?
Water conservatively. Sage tolerates drought well, but soggy soil causes rapid root rot. Always let the top few centimetres of soil dry out completely before adding water. Empty any saucers beneath pots so the container never sits in stagnant water.

### How can I propagate sage from cuttings?
Take semi-ripe heel or stem cuttings in July or August. Choose non-flowering shoots between seven and ten centimetres long. Strip lower leaves and plant the stems in sandy potting soil. Keep the soil lightly moist until roots establish, then pot them on individually.

## Benefits
Sage offers a bold, warm, camphor-like taste with slightly peppery and astringent notes. A little goes a long way. Crisp whole leaves in melted butter to create a rich sauce for gnocchi, ravioli, or green beans. It complements sweet, earthy vegetables like roasted pumpkin, butternut squash, and turnips. Add sage to poultry, pork, duck, and sausages to balance rich fats. You can also brew fresh leaves with honey and warm milk, or steep them for herbal tea.
