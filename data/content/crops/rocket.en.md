## Starting
Rucola is one of the easiest crops for Dutch balconies and small gardens. It loves cool conditions, unlike frost-tender crops that wait for IJsheiligen. You can sow outdoors starting in March, or even in February under glass or a cold frame. Sowings continue happily right through September or early October. For an ongoing supply from spring to late autumn, sow a small batch every two to three weeks. Sow seeds shallowly into light, moist potting compost. Germination happens rapidly, taking just five to fourteen days, even when soil temperatures are between eight and twelve degrees.

## Seedling
Direct sowing works best because pricking out disturbs sensitive roots and triggers premature flowering. Sow a few seeds per spot or thinly in rows. Once true leaves appear, thin seedlings using sharp scissors rather than pulling them up by hand. This snip protects the root system of your keeper plants. For square-foot containers, keep nine plants per square. In balcony window boxes, leave five to ten centimetres between individual plants.

## Vegetative
Rocket thrives in shallow balcony pots, window boxes, or fabric grow-bags with drainage holes. Keep containers in full sun during early spring and autumn. When high summer arrives between June and August, move pots into partial shade to avoid scorched foliage and bolting. Keep soil consistently moist at all times. Drought causes leaves to turn tough and unpleasantly bitter. Always water at the base of the stems rather than wetting the foliage. As a light feeder, standard potting soil with a thin layer of compost gives enough nutrition. Stems stay compact, so leaf crops require no plant supports.

## Flowering
Long summer days and heat trigger plants to bolt and produce flower stalks. Do not discard bolted plants immediately. The blossoms are fully edible, featuring a pleasant nutty, peppery flavour. Annual types produce creamy-white flowers with purple veins, while wild perennial varieties show bright yellow petals. They make lovely salad garnishes and attract visiting bees and hoverflies. If you let slender seed pods dry brown on the plant, you can collect fresh seeds in paper envelopes.

## Harvest
You can begin harvesting young rocket leaves just three to five weeks after sowing. Pick them using a cut-and-come-again method. Snip outer leaves two to three centimetres above soil level with clean scissors. Leaving the central growing core untouched lets the plant produce multiple fresh flushes. Younger leaves taste pleasantly mild and tender, whereas older leaves develop a sharper pungency. Enjoy leaves immediately for the best flavour. If storing, wash and spin dry, wrap in a slightly damp tea towel, and place in the refrigerator at two to five degrees for two to four days.

## FAQ
### Why do my leaves have tiny shotgun-like holes?
These holes come from flea beetles, known locally as aardvlooien. These jumping pests love warm, dry weather. You can protect your crop by keeping the soil surface consistently moist, as beetles dislike moisture. Using an ultra-fine insect mesh over balcony containers also prevents them from landing on leaves.

### Can I transplant rucola seedlings into bigger containers?
Direct sowing into the final pot is best. Pricking out or disturbing the root ball shocks young seedlings. That stress often causes the plant to bolt into flower prematurely. If you must adjust spacing, sow thinly and snip excess seedlings with scissors instead of transplanting them elsewhere.

### Why do my rocket leaves taste overly sharp and bitter?
Strong heat, direct midday sun, and dry soil cause rocket leaves to become unpleasantly pungent. Older leaves also taste sharper naturally. To keep flavours mild, keep container compost evenly damp, position pots in dappled light during warm summer months, and harvest leaves when they are young.

### How do I protect balcony seedlings from slugs?
Slugs are greedy eaters of tender spring greens. You can protect container seedlings by elevating your pots off the balcony floor or patio ground. Applying ecological ferric phosphate pellets around the base of pots also keeps slugs and snails away without harming the surrounding garden environment.

### What family does rucola belong to for crop rotation?
Rucola belongs to the brassica or cabbage family, not the lettuce group. In open garden beds, avoid planting it where cabbages, radishes, or turnips previously grew to prevent clubroot and soil-borne diseases. For balcony containers, using fresh potting soil or refreshed compost each season eliminates rotation worries.

### How do I harvest rocket so it keeps growing?
Use the cut-and-come-again approach. Cut only the outer leaves two to three centimetres above the compost using sharp kitchen scissors. Always leave the inner growing heart intact. The plant will rapidly generate fresh rounds of foliage, letting you harvest several times from a single container sow.

### Do rocket plants need heavy fertilising?
No, rucola is a light feeder. Standard potting compost mixed with a thin layer of organic compost supplies all the nutrition plants require. Avoid fertilisers high in nitrogen. Excess nitrogen can cause leafy brassica crops like rocket to accumulate unwanted nitrates in their leaves as they grow.

### When should I sow rocket outdoors in the Netherlands?
Rocket tolerates cool weather very well, unlike tender summer vegetables that must wait until after IJsheiligen in mid-May. You can sow seeds outdoors from March right through until September or early October. For an even earlier start, sow under glass or inside cold frames in February.

## Benefits
Rocket brings a nutty, peppery bite to home cooking. Toss fresh leaves into raw salads with olive oil, balsamic vinegar, tomatoes, toasted pine nuts, and shavings of aged cheese. For warm meals, fold leaves through pasta or scatter them over hot pizzas right after baking. You can stir raw leaves into mashed potatoes to make classic Dutch rucolastamppot. Alternatively, puree mature, pungent leaves with olive oil, garlic, toasted nuts, and hard cheese for a bold homemade pesto.
