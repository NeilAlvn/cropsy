## Starting
Indoor pre-sowing is best between mid-April and early May. Do not start too early. Warm-season plants grow quickly indoors and become weak, pale, and leggy if light or warmth are lacking. Pre-sowing in pots protects tender young plants from cold, wet ground and hungry slugs. Cucumber seeds need a warm spot between 20°C and 25°C to sprout. They usually germinate fast, popping up in three to six days. Although direct outdoor sowing is possible in late May or June once soil warms up, starting indoors gives your container garden a safe, sturdy head start.

## Seedling
Cucumber roots are delicate and dislike disturbance. Never prick seedlings out bare-root. Sow a single seed per individual pot, paper pot, or egg carton. If a seedling outgrows its home early, gently move the entire root ball into a larger container. Wait until IJsheiligen passes in mid-May before planting outside. Night frosts must be completely finished. Harden off seedlings over four to seven days. Place them in a sheltered, semi-shaded spot by day and bring them inside at night.

## Vegetative
Give each plant a container holding at least 15 to 20 litres with ample bottom drainage holes. Cucumbers are very thirsty plants. Never let the potting soil dry out entirely. Pour water directly onto the soil and roots, keeping foliage dry to prevent fungal issues. Ensure pots drain freely so roots do not rot. Feed these hungry vines every one to two weeks during peak fruiting using an organic potassium-rich fertiliser. Train stems vertically up trellises, canes, or spirals reaching two metres high. This saves balcony space, improves airflow, keeps crops clean, and stops rot. Pinch out unwanted lateral shoots.

## Flowering
Yellow blossoms appear neatly in leaf axils. Traditional outdoor varieties produce both male and female flowers, relying on pollinating insects. Female blooms have a visible baby cucumber swelling at their base. Modern snack types are predominantly female and set fruit without pollination. Protect these parthenocarpic container varieties from cross-pollination by insects carrying pollen from outdoor cucumbers or squashes. Cross-pollination can cause parthenocarpic fruits to develop hard seeds or an unwelcome bitter flavour.

## Harvest
Harvest cucumbers while they are young, tender, and crisp. Larger fruits lose their delicious flavour. Pick snack types when they reach 10 to 15 cm long. Always cut stems cleanly with a sharp knife or secateurs rather than pulling. Harvest two or three times weekly during peak summer. Frequent picking prevents plant exhaustion and triggers the growth of new fruit. Never store fresh cucumbers in the refrigerator. Cold air causes sogginess and rapid damage. Keep them in a cool, dark cellar or pantry around 13°C, away from ethylene-releasing ripe tomatoes. They will stay fresh for ten days.

## FAQ
### Why do baby cucumbers turn yellow and drop off?
Container cucumbers often drop small fruits when moisture levels fluctuate sharply. A brief drought triggers fruit drop as an emergency survival response. Cold night temperatures below 12°C to 14°C or nutrient shortages also prompt the plant to shed young cucumbers. Keep watering steady and ensure adequate feeding.

### What causes cucumbers to taste bitter?
Bitterness is usually caused by sudden environmental stress. Common triggers include dry spells, drought, and abrupt cold snaps. Unwanted cross-pollination of parthenocarpic cultivars by insects carrying pollen from squash or traditional varieties also causes bitterness. Maintain consistent soil moisture and mulch container surfaces to keep soil temperatures stable.

### How do I manage powdery mildew on cucumber leaves?
Powdery mildew appears as a white dusting on foliage in late summer. Protect your plants by ensuring good ventilation and vertical vine support. Always apply water directly to the soil to keep the leaves dry. Remove heavily infected older leaves promptly to reduce fungal spread around your balcony garden.

### How can I control red spider mites on a balcony?
Red spider mites thrive in warm, dry spots, especially near sunny brick walls. They cause speckled yellow leaves and fine webbing. Mist or spray the undersides of leaves with water to deter them. You can also encourage helpful natural predators like ladybirds and predatory mites on your balcony.

### How do I protect young cucumber plants from slugs?
Slugs can strip tender seedlings down to bare stems overnight after planting out. Shield young plants by placing protective collars around their bases. You can also elevate your pots on plant stands or tables until the main vine stems thicken, toughen up, and resist damage.

### Should I prick out cucumber seedlings?
Avoid bare-root pricking out because cucurbit roots are exceptionally fragile and resent disturbance. Instead, sow a single seed into its own individual pot, paper pot, or egg carton. If the seedling outgrows the container before planting out, gently shift the entire undisturbed root ball into a larger pot.

### How often should I fertilise container cucumbers?
Cucumbers are heavy feeders needing rich, compost-based soil. During peak fruiting, give container vines supplemental organic liquid feed every one to two weeks. Choose a feed high in potassium, such as organic tomato feed, to support steady flower set and continuous cucumber formation throughout the summer months.

### Why should cucumbers stay out of the fridge?
Refrigerators are far too cold for cucumbers. Chilly temperatures cause rapid cold damage, sogginess, and fast deterioration. Store freshly harvested cucumbers in a dark, cool pantry or cellar around 13°C. Avoid keeping them near ethylene-producing crops like ripe tomatoes to help them last for ten days.

## Benefits
Enjoy cucumbers raw, sliced into crisp summer salads, stirred into refreshing cold tzatziki, or packed whole into lunchboxes as quick snacks. For a traditional warm Dutch dish, make gestoofde komkommer by gently sautéing cucumber cubes with onions, herbs, mince or vegetarian mince, and fresh tomatoes. Cucumbers also preserve wonderfully. Make quick sweet-and-sour zoetzure komkommers with vinegar, mustard seeds, dill, and sugar, or ferment them in a two percent salt brine for tangy pickles.
