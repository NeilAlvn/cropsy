## Starting
Start lettuce indoors in pots or seed trays from late February or March. You can sow directly outdoors from March until September. Lettuce seeds need light to sprout, so cover them with just a minimal dusting of sieved soil or white sand. Seeds sprout best between 5 °C and 18 °C in two to fourteen days. Warm summer heat causes dormancy. Above 20 °C to 22 °C, seeds refuse to emerge. During hot months, sow indoors on a cool, north-facing windowsill. Sow a small batch every two to three weeks to enjoy an ongoing harvest without having all heads mature together.

## Seedling
As soon as seeds sprout indoors, move them to a cool, bright location like an unheated bedroom with daylight. Warmth and dim light cause weak, leggy stems. Thin seedlings down to one strong plant per pot once the first true lettuce leaves appear. Lettuce tolerates light night frost if properly hardened off. Move protected young plants onto your balcony from April under a mini-greenhouse or fleece. After mid-May, plants stay fully outside, though summer crops need afternoon shade.

## Vegetative
Potted lettuce thrives in containers with good drainage holes. Head lettuce needs generous space and five to seven liters of soil per plant. Grow loose-leaf types or mini-heads closer together, or grow baby leaves in shallow balcony troughs. Lettuce roots stay shallow, so maintain regular, consistent moisture. Always pour water onto the soil around the base. Never wet the heart or leaves, avoiding rot and fungal issues. Mix moderate compost into your potting mix. Avoid heavy nitrogen fertilizers, which produce weak leaves and ruin head formation. Place pots in full spring sun, but shift them to partial shade during warm summer months.

## Flowering
Summer heat above 20 °C, long days, or dry soil trigger bolting. The plant stretches upward into a central flower stalk and produces white milky sap. This turns the foliage bitter, tough, and leathery. Harvest your lettuce immediately if the center begins to rise. If allowed to bloom, yellow flower clusters develop. These flowers eventually form fluffy seeds resembling dandelion fluff. You can gather and dry these seeds on a warm, dry afternoon to save for next season.

## Harvest
Harvest your lettuce early in the morning when the foliage is crisp and full of moisture. For head lettuce, check that the center feels firm, then cut the head just above the soil line with a sharp knife. For loose-leaf varieties, pick the outer leaves once they reach ten to twelve centimeters long. Keep the growing heart untouched so the plant continues producing fresh greens for weeks. Fresh leaves lose their crunch quickly. Wrap unwashed greens in a damp tea towel or place them in a ventilated box inside your refrigerator crisper drawer. Use within a few days.

## FAQ
### How can I protect my potted lettuce from slugs?
Slugs are major pests for tender lettuce foliage. To protect balcony containers, stick copper tape around pot rims or raise planters off the floor. Head out in the evening to handpick slugs off plants. You can also sow red-leafed varieties like red oak leaf or Salanova, as slugs actively avoid red anthocyanin pigments.

### Why is my lettuce failing to form a solid head?
Lettuce fails to form firm heads for several reasons. High summer temperatures, overcrowded planting, or varieties poorly suited to the current season often prevent proper heading. Additionally, feeding your soil with too much nitrogen stimulates limp leaf growth rather than tight heads. Keep containers cool and provide balanced, moderate nutrients.

### How do I manage aphids on lettuce leaves?
Aphids frequently gather on the undersides of lettuce leaves or settle deep inside the tender heart. Instead of sprays, introduce companion flowers such as marigolds or pot marigolds nearby on your balcony. These flowers attract beneficial natural predators, including hoverfly larvae and ladybugs, which rapidly hunt down aphid colonies on your greens.

### How should I water potted lettuce to prevent rotting?
Always apply water directly to the soil surface around the plant base. Avoid splashing water onto the head, center heart, or leaves, as damp foliage invites downy mildew and ground rot like Sclerotinia. Ensure all pots have drainage holes, and leave adequate space between plants to allow fresh air circulation.

### Why are my lettuce seeds not germinating in summer?
Lettuce seeds suffer from thermo-dormancy when temperatures rise above 20 °C to 22 °C. In hot weather, seeds refuse to sprout. To overcome this, start your summer seeds indoors in a cooler room, such as by a north-facing window. Keep the potting soil lightly moist until the young seedlings successfully emerge.

### Does balcony lettuce need any support or staking?
Most standard lettuce plants stay low and compact, requiring no stakes or trellis. Only tall, upright varieties like Romaine lettuce or celtuce might require a small bamboo cane to keep them steady if they grow tall. Otherwise, let plants grow unsupported in well-drained potting compost.

### Can lettuce survive early cold spells on a balcony?
Yes, lettuce is relatively cold-tolerant and can endure light night frosts if hardened off gradually. While tender crops must wait until mid-May, early lettuce can move outside into pots as early as April. Simply protect plants with garden fleece or place them inside a mini-greenhouse during chilly nights.

### Why have my lettuce leaves turned tough and bitter?
Bitterness occurs when the plant bolts into flower due to summer heat, long daylight hours, or dry soil. As the central flower stem elongates, the plant produces bitter white sap, making leaves tough. Harvest heads immediately at the first sign of stretching before flowers appear.

## Benefits
Tender young lettuce leaves make a crisp, fresh base for mixed spring salads, lunch sandwiches, and wraps. Firmer varieties like Romaine or celtuce can be briefly stir-fried, braised, or wok-tossed. If your plants start to bolt and taste bitter, mash the raw leaves into a traditional Dutch potato stamppot right before serving. Any surplus harvest from your balcony containers transforms easily into a comforting, creamy lettuce soup or a refreshing green smoothie.
