## Starting
Start your aubergine seeds indoors early, ideally between late January and late March. These warm-season crops grow slowly and require an extended growing season before autumn chills arrive. Give your seeds consistent warmth to germinate well. A heated propagation mat or a sunny, south-facing windowsill at twenty to twenty-five degrees Celsius works perfectly. Use a light, free-draining seedling compost, perhaps mixed with clean sand or vermiculite. Keep the soil gently moist while waiting for signs of life. Sprouts usually appear within two to three weeks under these cozy indoor conditions.

## Seedling
Prick out your seedlings in April once their first true leaves appear. Gently lift each plant by its leaves instead of the fragile stem. Pot them individually into nine-centimeter pots filled with quality potting compost. Set them slightly deeper so the seed leaves sit just above the soil surface, which encourages extra root growth. Keep your young plants indoors in a warm, bright spot between eighteen and twenty-two degrees Celsius until settled spring weather arrives.

## Vegetative
Wait until mid-May after IJsheiligen to move containers outdoors, avoiding cold shocks that stunt growth. Choose a pot holding at least ten to fifteen liters with drainage holes. Plastic containers help retain moisture effectively on sunny balconies. Place your container against a sheltered, south- or southwest-facing brick wall that radiates heat. Compact patio varieties like 'Patio Baby' or 'Mini Egg' thrive best here. Water directly at the base to keep foliage dry. Support young stems early with sturdy bamboo canes or spiral stakes.

## Flowering
Aubergines bloom best when daytime temperatures reach twenty-two to twenty-eight degrees Celsius. Ensure nights stay above fifteen degrees Celsius to prevent flower drop. Because balconies can lack insects, gently tap or shake flowering stems at midday to release pollen. Feed plants every two to three weeks with a potassium-rich liquid tomato feed to encourage sturdy fruit development. Remember that the lovely purple flowers belong to the nightshade family and are strictly toxic, so never eat them.

## Harvest
Harvest your aubergines from late July through October. Pick fruits when the skin shows a rich color with a soft, satin gloss. If it has a mirror shine, it is underripe; if dull and matte, it is overripe and spongy. Press gently with your thumb—the flesh should spring back lightly. Cut the fruit using clean shears, leaving the calyx attached. Watch out for the sharp prickles on the stem. Never store harvested aubergines inside the refrigerator, as cold damages them. Keep them at room temperature or in a dry pantry for up to one week.

## FAQ
### Why are my aubergine flowers falling off without setting fruit?
Cold weather is usually the main cause. Night temperatures dropping below fifteen degrees Celsius frequently trigger blossom drop. Additionally, sheltered balcony plants might miss natural insect activity or wind movement. Try gently tapping or vibrating the flower stems around midday to distribute pollen, and keep containers in the warmest sheltered location available.

### Can I put my container outside in April?
Keep your containers indoors through April. Aubergines are very sensitive to cold, and early spring chills stunt their development. Wait until mid-May, after the IJsheiligen period passes and frost risk is gone. Maintain seedlings inside under bright light and warm temperatures around eighteen to twenty-two degrees Celsius until outdoor conditions stabilize.

### How do I tell if an aubergine is ready to harvest?
Look for a soft satin gloss on firm, richly colored skin. A hard mirror shine signals the fruit is still underripe, while a dull matte appearance means it has become overripe and spongy. Press gently with your thumb; the flesh should yield slightly and spring back.

### Why are the leaves on my aubergine plant drooping?
Drooping leaves usually point to moisture stress in your container. Check your pot drainage immediately. Waterlogged roots suffocate quickly, while small pots can dry out completely within hours on warm, sunny afternoons. Ensure containers have drainage holes and keep the soil evenly moist by watering at the base.

### How should I store freshly harvested aubergines?
Never store freshly picked aubergines in your refrigerator. Temperatures below ten degrees Celsius cause chilling injury, spoiling the texture and flavor. Instead, keep them in a dry pantry or at room temperature between ten and fifteen degrees Celsius. They will remain fresh and firm for up to a week.

### Can I eat aubergine flowers like courgette flowers?
No, you must never consume aubergine blossoms. While courgette flowers are culinary treats, aubergines belong to the nightshade family, and their purple flowers are toxic. Leave the blooms on the plant so they can develop into fruits, and admire their pretty appearance from a safe distance.

### Which varieties work best for balcony pots?
Full-sized aubergines demand lots of room and heat, making them tricky for compact containers. Compact patio varieties are specially suited for urban pots and balcony planters. Cultivars such as 'Patio Baby', 'Mini Egg', or 'Pukkie' provide great yields without overwhelming small spaces.

### How do I handle common pests like spider mites and whiteflies?
Warm, sheltered balcony corners can attract red spider mites and whiteflies. Inspect the undersides of the leaves regularly for early signs of infestation. Ensure proper air circulation around your containers, avoid crowding plants, and welcome natural beneficial predators to keep pest populations under control.

## Benefits
Aubergines are versatile in the kitchen but must never be eaten raw due to solanine, which causes digestive upset. Cooking thoroughly neutralizes this compound and turns the flesh wonderfully creamy. Slice and salt the flesh first to draw out moisture and bitterness before cooking. Roast, bake, or grill them for hearty Mediterranean dishes like ratatouille, moussaka, or Italian parmigiana. They also make fantastic oven bakes with garlic and tomatoes, or smoky blended dips like baba ghanoush served with cool yogurt.
