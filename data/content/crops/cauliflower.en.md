## Starting
Cauliflower offers several sowing windows. For spring crops, start seeds indoors or in a cold frame from February to March at 15 to 20 °C. Cool seedlings down to 12 to 15 °C quickly. Move them outside after IJsheiligen in mid-May to prevent premature flowering from cold shocks. The summer crop is the most reliable choice. Sow it from April to May, then move it out in June. For autumn crops, sow between June and mid-July. Traditional Dutch weeuwenteelt involves sowing in late September or early October, overwintering potted plants in a cold frame, and planting out in mid-March.

## Seedling
Sow sparsely to give young plants room. Once true leaves appear, prick seedlings out into individual 7 to 9 cm pots. Provide plenty of light and cool air. Excessive indoor warmth causes weak, elongated white stems that easily collapse. Seedlings are ready for outdoor planting when they reach 8 to 12 cm in height with 4 to 6 true leaves. Remember that plants develop a broad leaf span, needing at least 60 by 60 cm of room. They will quickly overcrowd standard 30 by 30 cm garden squares.

## Vegetative
Choose a container holding at least 20 to 25 liters, with a depth of 25 to 30 cm and proper drainage holes. Cauliflower feeds heavily. Fill your pot with humus-rich, moisture-retentive soil blended with mature compost, dried cow manure pellets, and agricultural lime to raise the pH. Maintain a steady water supply without fail. Soil in containers dries out fast, so check moisture levels daily during warm weather. Any dry spell halts development or creates deformed heads. Firm the soil well around the base, heaping compost around the root collar to anchor the plant against strong balcony winds.

## Flowering
The harvestable curd is an immature, fleshy flower head. Sunlight triggers photosynthesis, which discolors the white curd to yellow-brown or pink, making it loose and coarse. Protect it once it reaches tennis ball size. Bend one or two inner leaves inward over the developing curd, securing them loosely with twine or a clothespin. This blanching technique shields the delicate curd from direct light. Alternatively, grow modern self-wrapping hybrid varieties that naturally fold their inner foliage over the head.

## Harvest
Harvest your cauliflower while the curd remains dense, firm, and pure white, before individual florets begin separating. Cut through the stem beneath the head. Keep the protective outer wrapper leaves attached to prevent bruising and moisture loss. Fresh heads keep for only two to three days at room temperature. Stored inside the refrigerator crisper drawer with leaves attached, they last six to eight days. You can also blanch florets for two to three minutes before freezing, or preserve them in piccalilli. Leave the harvested plant in its container to encourage small secondary florets to sprout from the leaf axils.

## FAQ
### Why did my cauliflower head form prematurely when tiny?
Growth interruptions cause premature buttoning, producing curds only the size of a walnut. Common triggers include cold drafts below 10 °C during early development, dry container soil, or root disturbance while potting on. Protect plants from cold drafts and keep moisture consistent.

### How do I protect plants from caterpillars?
Caterpillars from the cabbage white butterfly and cabbage moth chew leaves and bore into developing curds. Always cover your containers with fine insect netting from the very first day outdoors. Routinely inspect the undersides of foliage to wipe away any clusters of yellow eggs.

### What causes plants to turn blue-grey and collapse?
This damage points to the cabbage root fly. Flies lay eggs at the stem base during spring, and larvae burrow into root systems. To stop them, wrap a protective cabbage collar flat around the stem base, or lay the rootball horizontally when transplanting so larvae starve.

### How do I prevent clubroot in small gardens?
Clubroot is a soil-borne slime mold causing swollen roots and wilting in sunshine. Always fill your containers with fresh potting soil. Add agricultural lime to maintain a soil pH above 6.5 to 7.0, and practice a strict four to six year crop rotation for brassicas.

### Can I grow cauliflower in square foot gardening boxes?
Standard square foot gardening boxes are unsuitable for this crop. A single plant requires a footprint of at least 60 by 60 cm. Planting it in a 30 by 30 cm square will crowd out neighboring squares and dominate the bed for several months.

### Why are my seedlings growing tall, white, and floppy?
This condition, known as fileren, happens when indoor seedlings receive too much heat and too little light. Keep your seedlings cool, ideally between 12 and 15 °C after germination, and place them in bright conditions so they develop thick, sturdy green stems.

### When is the safest time to plant outside in spring?
Wait until mid-May, after IJsheiligen, before planting early spring crops outdoors. Cold shocks and temperatures below 10 °C can trigger premature flowering and ruin the harvest. Ensure you properly harden off your potted plants before moving them to their permanent balcony location.

### What is traditional Dutch weeuwenteelt?
Weeuwenteelt, or widow cultivation, is an overwintering technique for extra-early spring harvests. Sow early varieties like Alpha or Record in late September or early October. Overwinter the young potted plants inside a cold frame, then transplant them into outdoor pots or garden soil in mid-March.

## Benefits
Cauliflower offers a fresh, mildly peppery, and sweet flavor when eaten raw. Shave florets into crisp salads, dip them fresh, or grate them into raw couscous and pizza crusts. Boil or steam florets for just three to five minutes to maintain a crisp bite and avoid strong sulfur aromas. You can also roast whole heads, slice them into steaks, or bake hearty gratins. For zero-waste cooking, chop the tender inner green leaves and solid core to steam or stir-fry alongside your florets.
