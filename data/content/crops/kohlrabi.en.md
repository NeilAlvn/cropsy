## Starting
Start your kohlrabi journey indoors from March until early April using seed trays or small pots. Keep them at room temperature, around 15 to 20 °C. Sprouts will appear in five to ten days. As soon as green shoots emerge, move them to a bright, cooler spot around 12 to 15 °C. This prevents leggy, floppy stems that collapse later. You can also sow seeds directly outdoors in containers from April through July. For an extra early spring crop, experienced growers sow under cold glass in September or October. It is a quick and satisfying cool-season brassica for small balconies.

## Seedling
Prick out seedlings into individual nine-centimeter pots when their first true leaves appear. Harden young plants off gradually so they adapt to wind and outdoor temperatures. Kohlrabi handles light night frost, but protect early April plants with garden fleece. Traditionally, unprotected planting in pots waits until after the Ice Saints in mid-May. Never plant seedlings too deep. Keep the root ball level with the soil surface. The stem bulb swells above ground. Planting too deep leads to rotting or deformed, pear-shaped stems.

## Vegetative
Choose containers holding at least seven to ten liters with drainage holes at the base. In a square-meter planter, you can grow four plants per standard square. Position your containers in full sun or light half-shade, aiming for four to six hours of sunlight daily. Water consistently right at the soil line, avoiding the bulb and foliage. Uneven moisture makes bulbs split open. Mix ripe compost or organic fertilizer pellets into the potting soil before planting. Avoid heavy nitrogen fertilizers, as they produce massive leaves while leaving the bulb tiny. Your sturdy plants need no stakes or extra support.

## Flowering
Kohlrabi is a biennial crop grown for its swollen stem rather than flowers or fruit. Do not let it bloom. Sudden cold spells below ten to twelve degrees or intense summer heat and drought trigger bolting. When the plant bolts, it sends up tall stalks with yellow flowers. All energy shifts into flower production, turning the bulb tough, stringy, and completely inedible. Always harvest your bulbs before any flower stalks begin to emerge from the center.

## Harvest
Harvest arrives about sixty to seventy days after planting. Pick the bulbs when they reach six to ten centimeters across, roughly the size of a tennis ball or apple. Do not wait for oversized globes, as late harvests turn woody, tough, and spongy. Slice through the taproot just below the bulb using a sharp knife. Cut off all leaves immediately so they do not drain moisture from the flesh. Stored in a perforated bag inside the refrigerator vegetable drawer, bulbs stay crisp for one to two weeks. Avoid freezing, which destroys the crisp texture.

## FAQ
### Why did my kohlrabi bulb split open?
Splitting happens when watering is uneven, such as heavy rain following dry soil. The inner flesh expands faster than the skin can stretch, causing cracks. Keep soil evenly moist and apply mulch to stabilize conditions. If a bulb splits, harvest and eat it promptly before rot sets in.

### How do I protect my plants from caterpillars?
Cabbage white butterflies lay eggs beneath the foliage, and their caterpillars quickly strip the leaves bare. On a Dutch balcony or garden, shield your containers using fine insect netting. Cover the plants early in the season so butterflies cannot reach the leaves to deposit their eggs.

### Why is my kohlrabi forming a pear shape instead of a round bulb?
Kohlrabi swells from the stem section just above the soil surface. If you bury the seedling too deeply during transplanting, the stem struggles to expand properly. This results in an elongated, pear-shaped bulb or stem rot. Always keep the root ball level with the surrounding soil.

### Can I eat the leaves of the kohlrabi plant?
Yes, do not throw away the tender heart leaves. Young foliage is completely edible and tastes delicious. You can chop the greens finely to braise them alongside the bulbs, wilt them like spinach in a skillet, or stir them directly into fresh vegetable soups.

### What container size do I need for balcony kohlrabi?
Use containers holding at least seven to ten liters per plant, with a depth and diameter of thirty centimeters. Ensure your pot has drainage holes at the bottom to let excess water escape freely. Stagnant moisture damages root development and ruins your crop.

### Can kohlrabi survive night frost in early spring?
Kohlrabi can handle light overnight frost, but prolonged or severe cold harms bulb development. If you plant out early in April, drape garden fleece over your pots during cold snaps. Gardeners traditionally wait until after the mid-May Ice Saints to leave container plants unprotected outdoors.

### Why are my bulbs woody and tough?
Woody texture happens when you leave the bulb on the plant too long. Harvest when bulbs are between six and ten centimeters across, roughly tennis ball size. Overly mature bulbs or plants that undergo drought stress quickly turn fibrous, tough, and spongy inside.

### Can I freeze fresh kohlrabi for long-term storage?
Freezing is not recommended because the vegetable loses its signature crisp texture and vibrant color when thawed. Instead, store unpeeled bulbs without leaves in a perforated plastic bag inside your refrigerator crisper drawer, where they keep fresh and crunchy for up to two weeks.

## Benefits
Raw kohlrabi tastes fresh, juicy, and nutty with a hint of radish. Cooked, it turns sweet and mild, resembling tender broccoli stems and cauliflower. Peel the tough outer skin generously. Enjoy it raw as crunchy carpaccio slices, grated into summer salads, or sliced into sticks for dipping. Toss matchsticks in lemon water to stop browning. You can also braise it in cream, roast it with herbs, or puree it into velvety soup. Steam the tender young leaves just like spinach.
