## Starting
Turnips thrive in the cool Dutch climate and handle light night frost with ease. You do not need to wait for the Ice Saints. Sow seeds directly outdoors into pots from March through April. A second sowing window opens from August until mid-September. Avoid the warm weeks between mid-May and late July. Always sow directly into your containers. Starting seeds in trays or transplanting damages the sensitive taproot, resulting in misshapen roots later on. With soil temperatures between 10 °C and 25 °C, your seeds will sprout in seven to fourteen days.

## Seedling
Turnip seedlings require a gentle touch. Never prick out or transplant them, as taproots are extremely fragile. Instead, thin your seedlings once they emerge. Gently pull or snip away the weakest sprouts. In containers or square-meter planters, aim for nine plants per thirty-by-thirty-centimeter patch. Do not discard those culled seedlings. Rinse them well and enjoy them instantly as tender young turnip greens. They provide a delicious, fresh bonus right from your first garden thinning.

## Vegetative
Choose containers, boxes, or fabric grow bags at least 15 to 20 cm deep. Fill them with an airy, well-draining potting medium free from stones or hard clumps, allowing roots to swell symmetrically. Water evenly and consistently. If soil swings between drought and heavy soaking, bulbs may crack or turn bitter and spongy. Turnips feed lightly. Avoid nitrogen-heavy fertilizers, which produce massive foliage at the expense of root growth. A bit of compost or potassium-rich organic feed at planting is ideal. Plants grow compact, reaching 25 to 40 cm tall, requiring no trellises or stakes.

## Flowering
Rising temperatures above 20 to 25 °C and the long days of June trigger premature flowering. When this happens, a yellow flower stalk shoots up. Flowering ruins the harvest because energy shifts away from the root. The bulb stops growing, becoming woody, fibrous, and unpalatable. To prevent this, grow turnips strictly in early spring or late summer. Select slow-bolting cultivars such as 'Platte Witte Mei' to keep your balcony crops crisp and sweet before heat arrives.

## Harvest
Enjoy two distinct harvests from one pot. After four to five weeks, snip outer leaves as tender turnip greens. Always leave the inner growing heart intact so roots keep developing. Harvest the bulbs after eight to twelve weeks. Pick them young, around the size of a golf ball to a tennis ball, roughly 5 to 8 cm across. Leaving them in the ground too long makes them pithy and dry. Wrap fresh greens in a damp cloth in the fridge for two to three days. Trimmed bulbs keep for weeks at 3 to 5 °C.

## FAQ
### Why are my turnip roots woody and tough?
Woody roots usually occur when plants are harvested too late, exposed to extreme summer heat, or subjected to irregular watering. When soil dries out and is subsequently soaked, roots suffer. For tender, juicy bulbs, pick them when they reach golf ball or tennis ball size. Keep watering even throughout the season.

### How do I deal with flea beetles on my balcony?
Flea beetles are small jumping insects that chew tiny pinholes in turnip leaves, especially during warm, dry spring weather. To stop them, keep your container soil consistently moist. Covering your crops with fine-mesh insect netting right from the start provides reliable physical protection against these pests.

### What causes tunneling inside my turnip roots?
Root tunnels are caused by the cabbage root fly. Adult flies deposit eggs at the base of the stems, and hatching white larvae bore directly into the bulb. Prevent this damage by securing fine-mesh netting over containers immediately after sowing, or fit a cabbage collar around each plant base.

### Can I transplant turnip seedlings?
No, avoid transplanting turnips. The bulb forms at the junction of the stem and taproot, making the root system very sensitive. Disturbing the roots during transplanting or pricking out leads to deformed, stunted bulbs. Always direct sow your seeds into containers and thin out the excess seedlings instead.

### Do turnips need support or staking in windy spots?
Turnips remain naturally low and compact, reaching a modest height of 25 to 40 cm. Because of their sturdy, bushy growth habit and underground root structure, they do not require any stakes, cages, or climbing supports, making them exceptionally convenient for exposed balconies or small patios.

### How do I protect young plants from slugs?
Slugs frequently devour tender turnip leaves during wet spring periods. Regularly check your containers and pick them off by hand. You can also place physical protective barriers around your pots to prevent slugs from crawling up into the foliage and destroying your emerging spring seedlings.

### Why did my turnip bolt and produce yellow flowers?
Turnips bolt when exposed to temperatures exceeding 20 to 25 °C and the lengthening daylight of late spring. Once the yellow flowers appear, the bulb becomes fibrous and woody. Avoid this issue by sowing early in spring or waiting until August for a late autumn harvest.

### How should I store autumn turnips for winter?
After an autumn harvest, trim off the green foliage completely. Store the cleaned bulbs in your refrigerator vegetable drawer at 3 to 5 °C for several weeks. For long-term winter storage lasting two to four months, pack them into a box of moist sand placed in a cool cellar.

## Benefits
Turnips are versatile in the kitchen. Raw young roots offer a crisp, peppery taste similar to radishes; slice them thinly into fresh salads without peeling. You can also roast them in olive oil, braise them, or mash them into soups and hearty Dutch stamppot. Do not overcook them to avoid an overly strong cabbage flavor. Turnip bulbs are also wonderful pickled or fermented. Do not overlook the greens: stir-fry them like spinach, fold them into warm dishes, or blend them with garlic, oil, and nuts into pesto.
