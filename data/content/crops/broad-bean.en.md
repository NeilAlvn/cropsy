## Starting
Broad beans love the crisp chill of early Dutch spring. Unlike tender summer beans, these hardy plants sprout at cool temperatures from 5 °C. They even shrug off light frosts down to -5 °C. Start them outdoors from February to mid-April. You can also sow winter-hardy varieties like ‘Aguadulce’ in autumn. Soak the thick seeds in tepid water for 8 to 12 hours before planting to speed up emergence. If you use a square-foot garden box, fit four plants per square grid. Give your container reliable drainage holes right from the beginning.

## Seedling
Pre-sowing indoors during January or February protects vulnerable seeds from hungry mice, birds, and freezing, waterlogged soil. Broad beans form a vigorous taproot early, so pick deep pots, root trainers, or cardboard rolls. Move the seedlings outside into a cool, sheltered corner immediately after germination to stop them from stretching. Plant them out into larger containers once they reach 5 to 10 cm tall. Drape horticultural fleece over them if severe late frosts hit your balcony.

## Vegetative
Windy balconies can easily topple tall plants. Pick compact dwarf varieties like ‘The Sutton’ that stay between 30 and 50 cm tall. Select a pot at least 25 to 30 cm deep holding 10 to 15 liters for one or two plants. Mound up soil around the stems when they reach 15 to 20 cm to strengthen their footing. Stake tall varieties with corner canes and twine. Keep the soil evenly damp, avoiding wet feet that rot roots. Never feed extra nitrogen fertilizer or heavy compost, as root nodules fix their own nutrients.

## Flowering
In April and May, fragrant, orchid-like blooms open with lovely black and white markings. Bees and bumblebees visit regularly to pollinate the blossoms. Watch out for sudden heat waves above 25 °C or drying winds, which cause flower drop. Once you spot four to six trusses of pods setting on a stem, pinch off the tender growing tip. This crucial step directs energy into swelling the lower pods while removing the soft shoots that sap-sucking aphids adore.

## Harvest
Harvest arrives between late May and early July, around two and a half months after spring sowing. Pick the pods when they feel plump and you can see the bean shapes bulging clearly inside. Do not wait until the skins turn leathery. Open a pod to check the scar where the bean attaches; it should be white or pale green, never black. Freshly shelled beans stay fresh in the refrigerator for two to three days. For longer storage, blanch shelled beans in boiling water for one to two minutes, cool in ice water, and freeze.

## FAQ
### How do I stop black bean aphids on my balcony?
Sow early in February or March so pods develop before warm weather brings black bean aphids. Once lower pods set, pinch out the soft leafy shoot tips where aphids gather. You can also hose clusters off with water, apply a soft-soap and garlic spray, or plant summer savory nearby.

### Can I grow broad beans in a regular potting mix with compost?
Broad beans are legumes that make their own nitrogen via root nodules. Rich compost or nitrogen-heavy plant food is counterproductive. Too much nutrition produces huge masses of leafy growth at the expense of bean pods and attracts pests. Simple, well-draining soil with little to no added compost is best.

### Which broad bean varieties perform best in balcony containers?
Standard garden varieties grow up to 140 cm and blow over easily in balcony gusts. Choose compact dwarf varieties instead, such as ‘The Sutton’ or ‘Sutton Dwarf’. These stay between 30 and 50 cm tall, thrive in 10 to 15 liter containers, and require much less staking.

### What should I do if my seedlings become tall and spindly indoors?
Indoor warmth makes broad bean seedlings stretch rapidly. Move your seedling pots outside to a cool, sheltered balcony spot immediately after the seeds sprout. As long as temperatures stay above freezing, cool air promotes stocky, resilient growth. Plant them out when they are 5 to 10 cm high.

### Why are my broad bean flowers dropping off without setting pods?
High spring temperatures over 25 °C and dry, hot winds often trigger flower drop and poor fruit set. Broad beans are cool-season crops that need steady moisture while flowering. Keep the soil evenly damp, shelter pots from scorching balcony corners, and welcome visiting bumblebees to ensure successful pollination.

### How do I protect seedlings from unexpected late frosts?
Broad bean plants tolerate light frosts down to -4 °C or -5 °C without trouble. However, if an unusually severe freeze strikes after planting out, cover your plants temporarily with horticultural fleece. Remove the fleece once the cold snap passes to allow plenty of sunlight and fresh airflow.

### What are the reddish-brown spots appearing on my plant's leaves?
Reddish-brown spotting indicates chocolate spot disease, caused by cool, humid spring weather. Good air circulation is key to preventing moisture buildup on balconies. Avoid overcrowded containers, maintain good spacing between stems, and locate your pots in a bright spot where foliage dries off quickly after spring showers.

### What companion plants work best alongside container broad beans?
Sow summer savory or dill close to your broad beans, as they help deter black aphids. Nasturtiums also make an excellent companion by serving as an aphid trap crop. These companions fit easily around the edges of large containers or nearby balcony pots without crowding root systems.

## Benefits
Tender young broad beans cook in just five to eight minutes with summer savory, matching wonderfully with crispy bacon lardons or parsley cream. For larger beans, try double-podding: blanch briefly and slip off the pale outer skin to uncover vibrant green, sweet kernels for risottos and fresh salads. Do not throw away the leafy shoot tips you pinch out either. Sauté them gently with olive oil and garlic just like fresh spinach for a quick balcony treat.
