## Starting
Start seeds indoors between February and March on a warm windowsill. Stevia requires light to sprout, so never bury the seeds. Scatter them directly onto moist seedling compost and press gently into the surface. Add at most a light dusting of sieved compost or vermiculite. Maintain a steady temperature between 20 and 24 °C. Germination takes two to four weeks and stalls in cooler air. Because germination rates are naturally modest, patience is essential. You can also bypass seeds entirely by purchasing spring nursery plants or rooting summer tip cuttings in moist cutting compost.

## Seedling
Young seedlings grow slowly and need generous light. Place them on a south-facing windowsill or under grow lights to avoid stretched stems. Water gently from below or use a spray mister to protect fragile roots. When plants develop two or three pairs of true leaves, pot them individually into small pots. Keep them indoors until mid-May, after IJsheiligen has passed. Cold air damages young growth. Harden plants off outside over seven to ten days before potting them permanently outdoors.

## Vegetative
Potted cultivation warms quickly and lets you move plants away from harsh maritime winds. Pick containers with drainage holes holding at least five to seven liters. Fill with well-draining, sandy potting soil. Place pots in a sunny, sheltered corner against a warm brick wall. Stevia has shallow roots that hate both dryness and soggy soil. Keep the compost evenly moist. Feed lightly with organic herb or tomato fertilizer once every three to four weeks. High nitrogen weakens flavor. Pinch out growing tips early in summer to encourage bushiness. Stake taller stems with a thin bamboo cane.

## Flowering
Stevia blooms in late summer as days grow shorter. Clusters of small white flowers appear across the plant. Once flowering starts, stem growth slows down and the leaves turn slightly bitter. Pinch off early flower buds immediately to extend your sweet leafy harvest. Autumns in the Low Countries are too cold and damp for seeds to set outdoors. To collect viable seed umbrellas, move your container inside a warm greenhouse or bright indoor room to ripen dry.

## Harvest
Harvest your stevia from July through September. Sweetness peaks just before flower buds open fully. Pick fresh individual leaves whenever you need them throughout summer. For a major autumn harvest, trim stems back by two-thirds. Dry the cut sprigs upside down, or spread leaves on mesh trays. Choose a warm, dark, airy location out of direct sunlight so the leaves stay green. Once crisp and brittle, crush them by hand or grind them in a coffee grinder. Stored in dark, airtight glass jars at room temperature, the dried leaves stay potent for over a year.

## FAQ
### Can stevia survive a Dutch winter outside?
Stevia cannot tolerate freezing conditions. Move your container indoors before the first autumn night frosts arrive in October. First, cut the stems back to five or ten centimeters. Keep the pot in a cool, bright room between 10 and 15 °C throughout winter. Water very sparingly, giving just enough moisture to prevent complete drying without rotting the roots.

### Why did my seeds fail to sprout?
Stevia seeds lose viability rapidly, and standard seed batches often have low germination rates. Failure usually happens when seeds are planted too deeply or kept too cool. The seeds require light to wake up and must stay on top of the soil. They also need a steady warmth of 20 to 24 °C. Below this range, germination typically stalls.

### Why are my plant's leaves turning yellow?
Yellow leaves and collapsing roots point to waterlogging or poor container drainage. Stevia features shallow, fibrous roots that suffocate easily in soggy soil, particularly during chilly weather. Always check that your pot has adequate drainage holes and never let it sit in a saucer of standing water. Keep the potting compost consistently damp, never completely soaked.

### How do I protect plants from balcony pests?
Slugs eagerly target tender young seedlings in spring. Growing in pots helps lift plants away from ground slugs. In warm, sheltered balcony corners or against sunny walls, monitor your plants for aphids and spider mites. Regular inspections and proper airflow keep pest populations under control without stressing your leafy herbs.

### When should I move my container outdoors?
Wait until mid-May, after IJsheiligen has passed, before moving pots permanently onto your balcony or patio. Stevia is sensitive to cold soil and late frost. Introduce your plants to outdoor conditions gradually over seven to ten days. Place them in sheltered shade before giving them full sun against a warm wall.

### Why should I pinch back the growing tips?
Unchecked plants can grow tall and fragile, reaching up to eighty or one hundred centimeters. Heavy summer showers or balcony winds can snap these spindly stems. Pinching out the top growing tips early in the summer encourages strong lateral branching, creating a stocky, bushy plant with far more leaves for harvest.

### How often should I fertilize my container plant?
Stevia is a light feeder that thrives without rich compost. Feed your container every three to four weeks with a light dose of organic herb or tomato fertilizer. Avoid fertilizers high in nitrogen. Too much nitrogen stimulates soft, brittle growth and dilutes the sweet steviosides in the leaves, resulting in less flavorful harvests.

### Can I grow stevia from summer cuttings?
Yes, taking summer tip cuttings is an excellent alternative to starting from erratic seeds. Take fresh tip cuttings during the warm summer months and insert them into moist cutting compost. They root readily in warm, bright conditions. This provides vigorous new plants that retain all the sweet qualities of the parent plant.

## Benefits
Stevia leaves provide intense natural sweetness with a subtle liquorice undertone. Steeping one or two fresh or dried leaves sweetens an entire pot of tea. Pair them with fresh mint, lemon verbena, or blackcurrant leaves at eighty degrees. Simmer dried leaves in water to create a versatile sweet syrup that lasts two weeks chilled. Because steviosides are heat-stable, you can use dried leaves or ground powder in low-sugar stewed fruit, berry compotes, homemade ketchup, and custom herbal sugars.
