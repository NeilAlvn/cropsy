## Starting
Bush beans stay naturally compact, reaching 40 to 50 cm high without needing tall climbing structures. They are warmth-loving plants that cannot handle frost. Outdoor sowing on your balcony starts only after Ice Saints around mid-May, running through early or mid-July. To protect against seed rot in cold spring soil, bean flies, or hungry birds and mice, start seeds indoors from late April to early May. They germinate rapidly at room temperature, around 20 to 21 °C, typically within 7 to 10 days. Keep the seed compost lightly moist, never soaking wet, for a dependable start.

## Seedling
French beans have very sensitive roots and dislike disturbance. Avoid traditional pricking out entirely. Sow directly into individual small pots, soil blocks, or air-pots, or transplant seedlings very gently as soon as seed leaves appear. After Ice Saints passes in mid-May, take several days to harden off your young plants. Gradually introduce them to outdoor breezes, natural sunlight, and fluctuating temperatures before moving their containers outside permanently. Protect them carefully during these early days.

## Vegetative
Give your beans an airy potting soil with excellent drainage and a neutral pH. Use a pot of 10 to 15 liters with bottom holes for 3 to 5 plants, or place 9 plants in a 30 by 30 cm planter. Beans fix their own nitrogen via root nodules, so avoid nitrogen-rich fertilizers and fresh compost. Excess nitrogen creates dense foliage without pods. Water moderately at the base to keep foliage dry and prevent fungal issues. Earth up soil against the stem at the first leaf pair, or support bushy stems with twigs.

## Flowering
Bush beans are strictly self-pollinating, making them ideal for sheltered balcony corners without relying on visiting bees. They need a sunny spot providing 6 to 8 hours of sunshine daily. Extreme drought or sudden cold snaps can cause blossom drop, so maintain consistent, even soil moisture throughout this critical stage. From sowing to pod formation, the process moves swiftly. You can expect your very first harvest roughly 8 to 9 weeks after planting the seeds.

## Harvest
Pick pods while they are still young, slender, and tender. When seeds swell noticeably inside, the pods turn tough and stringy. Bush beans concentrate their yield across a short window of 2 to 3 weeks. Harvest at least once or twice each week to keep plants producing. To ensure continuous fresh beans until autumn, sow fresh containers every 2 to 3 weeks. Freshly picked beans soften fast, so store them in an open bag inside your refrigerator crisper for up to 3 or 4 days. For longer storage, blanch for 2 minutes and freeze.

## FAQ
### When can I safely sow bush beans outdoors?
Outdoor sowing begins only after Ice Saints around May 15, lasting until early or mid-July. French beans are warm-season crops that cannot tolerate cold nights or frost. Sowing too early in cold spring soil causes seeds to rot. Wait until the weather warms up reliably.

### Why should I start bean seeds indoors?
Starting indoors from late April to early May prevents seeds from rotting in cold outdoor soil. It also guards seedlings against bean fly maggots, mice, and birds. Seeds germinate swiftly indoors at around 20 to 21 °C, usually emerging within 7 to 10 days.

### Do bush beans require climbing trellises?
No, bush beans remain compact, reaching only 40 to 50 cm in height. Unlike climbing pole beans, they do not need tall frames. However, plants become top-heavy as pods develop. Earthing up soil around stems or inserting a few small twigs will keep them upright.

### How should I fertilize my French beans?
Do not provide nitrogen-rich fertilizer or fresh compost. As members of the legume family, bush beans partner with root nodule bacteria to capture nitrogen from the air. Giving extra nitrogen results in excessive green leaves with very few flowers and pods.

### How do I handle black bean aphids on my balcony?
Black bean aphids form dense colonies that suck plant sap and secrete sticky honeydew. Manage them by growing companion plants like nasturtiums or marigolds to lure them away, encouraging ladybugs, or gently rinsing the foliage with a mild soap spray before flowering starts.

### Why are flowers dropping off my plants without forming pods?
Flower drop usually results from extreme dry spells or sudden cold snaps during bloom. While bush beans are self-pollinating and set fruit without bees, they require steady care. Provide at least 6 to 8 hours of daily sunlight and maintain consistent, even soil moisture.

### What causes yellowing leaves on French bean plants?
A few yellow lower leaves simply indicate natural aging of the plant. However, if the entire plant turns yellow, it generally points to root suffocation from excess water in poorly draining soil or an overly acidic growing medium. Allow proper drainage and avoid overwatering.

### How do I protect young seedlings from outdoor pests?
Slugs and birds frequently damage vulnerable young bean shoots. Protect containers during their first weeks by covering them with netting, garden cloches, or clear plastic covers. Once seedlings establish strong stems and true leaves, they withstand outdoor conditions much more successfully.

## Benefits
Freshly harvested bush beans deliver a sweet, crisp flavour to everyday meals. Cook them traditionally with a knob of butter and nutmeg, toss them with fresh summer savory, or stir them into warm salads and oven bakes. Never eat them raw. French beans contain phasine, a natural lectin that causes severe stomach cramps and nausea. Boiling or steaming pods for at least 10 to 12 minutes breaks down this compound completely, leaving them perfectly tender and safe.
