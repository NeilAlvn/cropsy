## Starting
Pointed cabbage is a fast, tender heading cabbage. Start sowing indoors or in a greenhouse from February to March. Seeds sprout quickly at 15–20 °C. The moment green shoots emerge, move them to a bright, cool spot around 12–15 °C. This prevents tall, weak, leggy stems. You can also sow outdoors in May or June for an autumn crop. For an early spring harvest, practice overwintering, known as weeuwenteelt. Sow in August or September and keep plants under glass or fleece in cold frames. This brassica always appreciates rich nutrients, steady moisture, and good pest covers.

## Seedling
Prick out seedlings into individual small pots or plug trays once true leaves appear. Hardening off is essential for these young plants. Move them outdoors into partial shade over seven to ten days. Seedlings can tolerate light chills once acclimated. You can plant them out under protective fleece in April. If you garden in frost pockets or open containers without fleece, wait until IJsheiligen passes in mid-May. Allocate one cabbage per square-foot grid to allow proper space.

## Vegetative
Choose a patio pot holding at least 10 to 15 litres for each cabbage. Fill it with nutrient-dense, humus-rich soil that retains moisture. Blend in aged compost and a dressing of lime or rock dust to maintain a neutral to alkaline pH. Spitskool feeds heavily. Mix in organic fertilizer pellets like dried cow manure. You can apply nettle tea during early leaf growth, but stop feeding once the central head starts forming. Keep watering steady; dry spells followed by sudden soaking will cause splitting. Earth up soil around the stem to shield against strong winds.

## Flowering
Pointed cabbage is grown purely for its tender vegetative cone, not flowers. Extreme conditions trigger premature bolting. Prolonged cold spells below 10 °C after planting out, or severe summer heat and drought, can cause heads to stretch. The central cone loosens into a tall yellow flower stalk, leaving leaves bitter and fibrous. Because brassicas easily cross-pollinate with wild mustard, kale, or broccoli, saving seeds in small gardens is difficult. Remove any bolted plants promptly.

## Harvest
Expect a harvest 70 to 90 days after planting out. Feel the cone gently; cut it with a sharp knife above the bottom leaves once the heart feels firm and dense. If heavy rain threatens mature heads, prevent splitting with a simple trick. Twist the cabbage slightly or slice half the roots with a spade to throttle water uptake. Tender spitskool leaves store for one to two weeks unwashed in a cool cellar, shed, or vegetable crisper. For long-term preservation, shred your heads for homemade sauerkraut or blanch and freeze the leaves.

## FAQ
### How do I protect plants from cabbage white butterflies?
Cabbage white butterflies quickly lay eggs on the leaves, and hungry caterpillars can strip plants bare. Makkelijke Moestuin and IVN note that physical exclusion is the only reliable non-chemical defense. Cover your container completely with fine insect netting, using a mesh size of 1.3 millimetres or less, or place a cage over your pots from planting day right through to harvest.

### How do I stop cabbage root fly attacks?
Cabbage root flies deposit their eggs directly at the base of the stem, allowing larvae to feast on young roots. You can stop them by fitting rubber or cardboard collars closely around the stem. Another effective technique recommended by Velt is horizontal planting, known as plat planten, which disrupts the normal egg-laying habits of the fly and shields the root system.

### Can I avoid clubroot when growing on a balcony?
Yes, balcony containers offer a major advantage against clubroot. This destructive soil-borne fungus deforms and swells root systems in open ground. Because potted plants grow in fresh, clean potting compost, you sidestep the fungus entirely. In garden beds, you would need lime additions and a strict six-year crop rotation to suppress this disease, making pots a hassle-free choice.

### Why did my cabbage head split open?
Heads split when moisture levels swing wildly. If a dry spell is followed by heavy watering or rain, the core takes up water too quickly and bursts outward. Keep container soil consistently damp to avoid growth checks. If heavy rain is forecast over mature heads, twist the cabbage slightly to sever some roots and reduce sudden moisture absorption.

### How should I feed my cabbage in a pot?
Spitskool is a heavy feeder that needs rich nutrition throughout its cycle. Mix aged compost and organic fertilizer pellets, like dried cow manure, into the potting soil before planting. You can supply liquid nettle tea during early leaf growth. However, Tuinadvies warns you must stop liquid feeding once the head starts forming to avoid loose, leafy cabbage heads.

### What is weeuwenteelt?
Weeuwenteelt is an overwintering cultivation method used for an ultra-early spring harvest. Seeds are sown in August or September, then protected through the cold months under fleece or glass cold frames. This method gives plants a head start as temperatures rise in early spring, yielding sweet, crisp pointed heads weeks ahead of standard spring-sown crops.

### How do I deal with slugs on young plants?
Tender cabbage transplants are prime targets for slugs during damp Dutch spring weather. To protect your young crops until they are established, install protective barriers around container edges. You can also cover vulnerable seedlings with cloches or set up beer traps nearby. Once plants grow larger and tougher, slug damage becomes much less of a threat.

### Why did my cabbage start bolting into flowers early?
Premature bolting happens when plants face harsh temperature stress. Extended cold snaps below 10 °C shortly after early spring planting can shock the cabbage. Severe heatwaves and dry soil during summer also trigger flowering. The plant responds by producing a yellow floral stalk instead of a dense cone, making the foliage tough and bitter.

## Benefits
Pointed cabbage offers a mild, sweet flavour and tender texture that cooks faster than round cabbage. Enjoy it raw in crunchy salads and slaws tossed with lime and peanuts. Its thin leaves cook in just three to five minutes in a hot wok, pairing wonderfully with Indonesian seasonings like ketjap, sambal, and ginger. You can also braise it gently in butter, fold it into traditional Dutch stamppot, bake it in savoury quiches, or add it to slow-cooked meals.
