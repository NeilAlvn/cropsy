## Starting
Sow your winter purslane directly outdoors from mid-August through late September. In the Dutch autumn, cool weather gives the best results. Under a cold frame or greenhouse glass, you can keep sowing well into October. Cool soil temperatures between 10 °C and 18 °C trigger germination in about 5 to 10 days. Avoid starting seeds during hot midsummer days because heat dormancy stops them from sprouting. Sow the tiny seeds thinly into shallow drills or balcony pots. In a compact square garden tray, nine pinches of a few seeds work wonderfully. Spring sowing in March is also possible.

## Seedling
Young winter purslane sprouts quickly with delicate, shallow root systems. Because these roots are fragile, avoid pricking out or transplanting individual seedlings. Sowing directly where they will grow delivers the happiest plants. You do not need to thin them heavily. The greens naturally form compact, low-growing clumps. If you sowed too thickly in a window box, gently thin leaving small gaps between tufts. This gives each leafy rosette enough room to expand across the container soil.

## Vegetative
This hardy winter green thrives in shallow balcony troughs with good drainage holes. Good drainage prevents winter rot during wet spells. Keep the potting soil evenly moist during the autumn growth spurt. Dutch rainfall usually provides plenty of moisture for exposed pots. If your container sits under a balcony overhang or glass cover, water lightly so roots never dry out. Winter purslane is a light feeder. Regular potting soil or compost provides plenty of nutrients. Avoid high-nitrogen fertilizers, as they cause weak leaves and excess nitrate buildup in dim winter light. The ground-hugging rosettes need no physical staking or support.

## Flowering
Rising temperatures and longer days in spring bring blooms. Upper leaves fuse into circular saucer shapes around the stems. Delicate white flowers emerge from the leaf centers, followed by glossy black seeds. Ants often carry these seeds away thanks to attached nutrient bodies. The plants naturally finish their life cycle by late spring. Clear your balcony containers by IJsheiligen in mid-May. This frees up warm pot space for tender summer crops like bush beans, peppers, or tomatoes.

## Harvest
Harvest crisp leaves with clean scissors, cutting stems just above the soil level. Always leave the growing center intact so new shoots appear. You can enjoy two to four harvests between November and April. Never harvest or touch leaves while they are frozen stiff during a frosty morning. Wait until the midday sun thaws them completely, or the damaged tissue turns black and rots. Fresh succulent leaves wilt rapidly once picked. Eat them straight away for the crispest texture, or store them dry in a sealed fridge container for two to three days.

## FAQ
### How does winter purslane handle severe Dutch frost?
Winter purslane is exceptionally hardy and survives cold drops down to minus fifteen degrees without damage. While the foliage may freeze solid on icy nights, it recovers quickly once daytime temperatures rise. Just remember never to harvest or disturb the leaves while frozen, as handling frozen foliage causes tissue rot.

### How is winter purslane different from summer purslane?
They come from completely different plant families despite sharing a common name. Summer purslane loves warm sunshine and perishes with the first autumn frost. In contrast, winter purslane thrives in cold weather down to minus fifteen degrees. Its flavor is also much milder, crisp, and far less acidic or slimy than summer varieties.

### Can I prick out and transplant young seedlings?
Transplanting individual seedlings is not recommended for this crop. The young plants develop delicate, shallow root systems that suffer severe shock when moved. Direct sowing your seeds straight into your final balcony boxes or garden containers produces much healthier growth, saving you extra work during the autumn planting window.

### Why should I clear the plants away around mid-May?
Winter purslane finishes its natural life cycle and dies back as late spring temperatures rise. Clearing containers around the IJsheiligen period in mid-May creates empty pot space at the perfect moment. This allows you to pot up frost-sensitive summer crops, such as patio tomatoes, peppers, and beans, as soon as frost dangers end.

### How do I prevent winter purslane from self-seeding aggressively?
If left to flower and drop seed, this plant readily colonizes nearby pots, patio pavers, and garden beds for years. Ants also help spread the seeds across outdoor areas. To keep your balcony tidy, harvest heavily and pull the plants out completely as soon as small white blooms appear in spring.

### What causes fungal leaf rot on indoor or covered plants?
Fungal rot and damping off happen most often when plants grow under glass, cloches, or cold frames with stagnant, humid air. Without airflow, botrytis quickly attacks moist foliage. Prevent leaf melt by opening frames and providing fresh ventilation on mild winter days, and ensure balcony pots have free-draining base holes.

### Do slugs cause problems for winter purslane?
Slugs rarely bother winter purslane during the cold autumn and winter months. Because low temperatures suppress most garden pests, this leafy crop remains virtually trouble-free on a Dutch balcony. You can grow lush green harvests without worrying about typical pest damage common to summer salads.

### What kind of fertilizer should I feed my containers?
Winter purslane is a very light feeder that needs very little supplementary food. Normal potting compost contains all the nutrients needed for a complete winter cycle. Avoid high-nitrogen fertilizers entirely. Heavy feeding produces weak, watery leaves and causes unwanted nitrate accumulation within leaf tissue under dim winter skies.

### Why did my seeds fail to sprout during midsummer?
Warm weather causes winter purslane seeds to enter heat dormancy. If you sow into warm soil during midsummer heatwaves, the seeds refuse to germinate. Always wait until mid-August or September when soil temperatures settle between 10 °C and 18 °C to ensure rapid, dependable germination in your containers.

## Benefits
Winter purslane brings a crisp, tender bite to cold-season kitchens. Its flavor is mild and succulent with subtle nutty undertones. Enjoy it raw as a salad base with walnuts and goat cheese, or layer it into sandwiches. The round flower disks and tiny white blossoms provide an edible garnish. For classic Dutch comfort food, stir raw leaves through hot mashed potatoes just before serving stamppot. You can also wilt it into warm pasta or purée it into rich winter soups.
