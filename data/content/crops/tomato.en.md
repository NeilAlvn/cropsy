## Starting
Start seeds indoors between mid-March and early April. Sowing earlier in Dutch late winters causes weak, leggy seedlings from low light. Use light, well-draining seed-starting compost or peat-free coconut mix with low nutrients. Space seeds two to three centimeters apart in trays or sow one to two per module. Keep the soil warm between 20 °C and 25 °C near a radiator or warm windowsill. Cover the tray with plastic wrap or a clear lid to lock in humidity. Sprouts will appear in seven to fourteen days.

## Seedling
Prick out seedlings into individual pots once their first true leaves appear, about two to four weeks after sowing. Plant them as deep as possible, burying the stem right up to the lowest leaves. The stem will quickly develop sturdy adventitious roots. Move the pots to your brightest window and lower the temperature to between 16 °C and 20 °C. Rotate the containers a quarter-turn each day to prevent seedlings from stretching toward the light.

## Vegetative
Harden off plants in early May over five to seven days before placing them outside after IJsheiligen in mid-May. Choose containers holding at least ten to fifteen liters with drainage holes. Fabric or plastic pots retain moisture better than untreated terracotta. Water directly at the stem base or using an inverted bottle, never wetting the leaves. Provide sturdy stakes or spiral canes for tall varieties. Enrich the soil with compost and apply organic tomato fertilizer every one to two weeks. Pinch out side shoots weekly from leaf axils. In mid- to late August, pinch out the top growing tip above the fourth or fifth fruit cluster.

## Flowering
Tomato flowers pollinate themselves with help from outdoor breezes and bumblebees. If your balcony or loggia is sheltered, gently shake or tap the support stakes daily around midday to release pollen. As fruit clusters form, pick off yellowing lower foliage. Prune leaves that block sunlight from reaching the tomatoes. This simple defoliation improves air circulation around the stems and helps the developing fruit ripen in direct sun.

## Harvest
Pick ripe tomatoes from July through October. Harvest by lifting the fruit and pressing gently with your thumb on the stem joint until it snaps off cleanly. Before the first October frosts arrive, harvest any remaining mature green fruit. Place them indoors inside a paper bag or cardboard box between 16 °C and 20 °C to ripen, adding an apple or banana to speed up the process. Never store tomatoes in the refrigerator, as cool temperatures ruin their aroma and cause mealy flesh. Keep them in a pantry or at room temperature between 12 °C and 18 °C.

## FAQ
### Why are my indoor seedlings growing tall and spindly?
Seedlings stretch when they experience too much heat combined with insufficient daylight, especially if started too early in late winter. Keep them on the brightest windowsill possible after germination and reduce ambient temperatures to between 16 °C and 20 °C. Rotate pots daily so they grow upright.

### What causes black sunken patches on the bottom of my tomatoes?
This condition is blossom end rot, which stems from erratic watering rather than poor soil quality alone. When container soil dries out completely, the plant cannot transport necessary calcium to the developing fruit tips. Keep potting soil evenly moist and check your pots every day during hot summer spells.

### Why are my tomatoes splitting open on the vine?
Fruit cracking happens when plants receive a heavy surge of water following a prolonged dry spell. The tomato pulp swells faster than the outer skin can stretch, causing it to split. Prevent this by maintaining steady, daily watering routines and avoiding drastic moisture swings in your containers.

### How do I protect balcony tomatoes from Phytophthora?
Tomato blight thrives in cool, damp summer conditions. Shelter your pots beneath a roof overhang, keep them strictly separated from potato plants, and always water at the soil level rather than over the leaves. Choose resilient outdoor varieties and remove any spotted or infected foliage immediately to halt spread.

### Do I need to remove side shoots on all tomato plants?
Only indeterminate or cordon varieties require regular side shoot removal, known as suckering or dieven. Check leaf axils weekly and snap off these small shoots so the plant channels energy into the main stem. Dwarf or bush varieties do not require suckering and should be left to grow naturally.

### Why should I pinch off the top of the tomato plant in August?
In the outdoor Dutch climate, late blossoms will not receive enough warmth or sunlight to ripen into harvestable fruit before autumn frosts arrive. Pinching the main growing tip in mid- to late August focuses the plant's remaining energy on ripening the four or five established fruit clusters.

### Why must I wait until mid-May to plant tomatoes outside?
Tomatoes are extremely sensitive to cold and cannot survive frost. In the Netherlands, late frosts are common until IJsheiligen around mid-May. Temperatures consistently dropping below 10 °C will stunt root and stem growth, so keep plants indoors or harden them off gradually until this date passes safely.

### Which container materials work best for small gardens or balconies?
Use plastic or fabric pots with drainage holes holding at least ten to fifteen liters of potting mix. Untreated terracotta containers dry out much too quickly on sunny balconies, creating severe moisture stress. Sturdy containers provide adequate root volume and make it easier to maintain balanced soil moisture throughout summer.

## Benefits
Homegrown garden tomatoes offer vibrant sweetness and bright acidity that store-bought fruit cannot match. Bite-sized cherry varieties make excellent garden snacks and add fresh flavor raw in summer salads. When warm summer weeks deliver a harvest glut, simmer the excess fruit into rich homemade pasta sauces or smooth passata. You can also roast tomatoes in the oven with fresh basil, oregano, and thyme, or freeze concentrated sauces to enjoy garden flavors right through the winter months.
